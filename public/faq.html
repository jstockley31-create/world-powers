Getting started

Can other players see my leader name?

No. Your leader name is private: other players only ever see your nation's name (or "New leader" before you found a nation). You see your own leader name on your profile, and the game staff can see it for moderation. To mention someone in chat, type @ followed by their nation's name.

Is there World Powers merch?

Yes. Official shirts are at worldpowersgame.com/shop, or tap Shop at the bottom of the side menu in the game. The shop is run by Printful: each item is printed when you order it and shipped straight to you, and Printful handles payment and delivery. For now it ships to US addresses only.

What is World Powers?

A free, real-time strategy game played in your browser with everyone else at the same time. You lead one nation on a real map of the world: run its economy, trade, form alliances, vote in the World Council, hold elections — and decide when it's worth going to war.

How do I start?
  • Create an account (one per email address) and you'll be taken to Found your nation.
  • Tap an unclaimed province on the globe — it must be at least two provinces away from any other nation.
  • Pick a name, a color and a form of government, then tap Establish nation.
  • Go to Operations and tap Collect Taxes. The Getting started checklist on your Overview walks you through the rest.
Can I try it without making an account?

Yes — tap Play as a guest on the home page or the sign-in screen. You get a full nation straight away. To keep it on any device and use mail, open the Account page and tap Save your account (add an email and password). Guest games that are never saved are deleted after 7 days without play.

Where should I start my nation?

Location matters, because resources follow the real world:

Rivers add 5 food, mountains add 4 steel and coasts add 3 goods. Mountains (+25%) and rivers (+15%) are easier to defend, and a coastal start gives you 2 warships and access to the sea.

Am I safe when I'm new?

Yes. New nations are protected from attack for 48 hours (unless they attack someone first). After that, you still can't be attacked by a nation that is much stronger than you — see War.

What happens while I'm offline?

Your nation keeps producing for 2 hours after you were last online, then pauses until you return. Nothing is taken from you for being away, but others can still attack you if you're at war.

What is the login streak?

Claim International Aid (in Operations) every day to build a streak. Each day in a row adds +15% to the grant, up to ×1.75 — and every 7th day pays ×2.5 plus bonus experience. Claim within 48 hours of your last claim to keep the streak going. Your streak is shown on the Overview.

What happens when I come back after a while?

If you've been away for an hour or more, a While you were away summary shows attacks on you, unread mail, decisions waiting, notifications and what happened to your nation.

What are the nations marked “AI”?

Computer-controlled nations, each with a personality (merchant, expansionist, warlord or isolationist). They play by the same rules as people (except the Rising Power, below), vote in the Council, can be traded with, allied, attacked — and conquered. They will also declare war on real players whose armies they can match. They gradually step aside as more real players join.

They fight like players too: they attack neighbors of similar strength (never much weaker or brand-new nations), hit back at anyone who attacks them — even from overseas — with invasions, missiles and air strikes, gang up on rogue states, and refuse to trade with nations the World Council has condemned or sanctioned.

What is the Rising Power?

One AI nation, marked ⚠ Rising Power, is a superpower the world has to stop together. It is honest about being helped: foreign backers keep its money, technology and military growing toward about 95% of the strongest player's.

Fighting it is free. Attacking it adds no aggression, and a war with it has no war upkeep and no GDP, unemployment or unrest penalty. When it rises, everyone is told in World Chat and on the Overview page.

How to beat it: the damage you do sticks. It can't rebuild for 1¾ hours after it's attacked or struck, and once 3 or more nations are at war with it, its backers pull out completely: no more money or weapons, and its forces slowly wear down. Its air force can never be more than 1.75× the biggest player air force, and its fortifications at most 3 levels above the best-fortified player. Alone you'll struggle; together you can bring it down.

  • It declares war on the strongest players, keeps a navy and air force able to reach them, and invades (by land, sea or air), fires missile salvos and launches air strikes. Even when it isn't looking for a fight, it hits back at anyone attacking it.
  • It builds a nuclear deterrent, then a launchable warhead, and will fire it at a player it has been at war with for at least 6 hours.
  • AI nations don't vote in the World Council, so players have to show up and vote: nuclear inspections can dismantle its warhead while it is being assembled, and sanctions or an embargo slow it down.
  • Team up: a Collective Defense Pact brings every member into the war when one is attacked.
  • A nuclear deterrent normally protects a capital from siege, but not a rogue state's. Once the Rising Power launches a nuke or is sanctioned into a rogue state, its capital can be taken and the nation destroyed.

When it falls, the whole world is told, and another power may rise after a break (48 hours unless the admins change it). Admins can also raise one early, tune its strength or turn it off.

Nuking it. You can launch an armed warhead at the Rising Power while at war with it. It is treated differently from other strikes: only +25 aggression, no fallout, no rogue status, your allies stay and your taxes keep coming. The warhead is spent, but it comes back armed once that Rising Power falls. You can nuke a Rising Power once every 7 days.

Playing on a phone

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. The main pages are in the bar at the bottom of the screen; everything else is under More (Military, Economy, Government, Intelligence, Diplomacy (with the World Council at the top), Mail, Rankings, Guide, FAQ and Account).

How do I install it like an app?

Tap More → Install as an app. It then opens full-screen from your home screen with no browser bars.

  • Android (Chrome): the button installs it directly, or use the ⋮ menu → Install app.
  • iPhone: open the game in Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen → Add.

How do I get notifications on my phone or computer?

Everything that happens to you appears under the bell at the top — tap × to dismiss one, or Clear all. Tap the bell at the top → Turn on phone & desktop notifications, then allow them. You can choose exactly which alerts you get (attacks, war declarations, mail, elections, operations ready, and more). On iPhone, notifications only work after installing the game to your Home Screen.

How do I rank up?

Every operation and decision earns experience (XP), and so do victories, projects, world crises, the World Games and achievements. Each rank adds 5 administration capacity (how much land you can run), +10% production, +8% military strength and +3% tax revenue, more agents, higher drill levels, more law slots and a bigger daily aid.

Every promotion also pays a one-time reward (from $6M at Mayor to $150M at World Power) plus +5 approval and stability, and shows what the new rank unlocks. Your progress to the next rank is the gold bar in your nation card, and the Rank panel on the Overview page lists your bonuses.

Chieftain (0) → Elder (100) → Mayor (300) → Governor (700) → Senator (1,500) → Prime Minister (3,000) → President (6,000) → Sovereign (12,000) → Emperor (25,000) → Supreme Leader (50,000) → World Power (100,000).

At Mayor you can declare war and propose World Council resolutions.

How are nations ranked?

The Rankings page orders every nation by its score, which adds up:

  • 100 per province
  • 0.4 per soldier, 8 per warship and 6 per aircraft
  • technology (each level counts a little less than the one before)
  • 0.1 per XP, 1 per 2,000 people, and 1 per $500,000 of GDP

You can also rank by economy, society or military. The page charts the race over the last day, three days or week, and shows each nation's share of world GDP, military power and land, the biggest movers, and how many places every nation rose or fell since yesterday. History is recorded once an hour.

From Saturday, November 21 (when Season 1 ends), rankings use a fairer score:

  • Your rank uses your average score over the last 12 hours, so steady play counts and nobody jumps to the top in one lucky hour.
  • GDP, army and fleets still count, but each extra billion or 100,000 troops counts a little less, so balanced nations do best.
  • Effort counts more: 500 per province, development levels, wins against real players (opponents at least half your strength, up to 3 a day against the same nation), aid and disaster relief you give, and allies and alliance-team mates you keep for a day or more.

Season 1 itself is finished and paid out under the old score.

Can I send aid or trade with an account on the same network?

No. Aid, trade offers and disaster relief between accounts that play from the same home network are blocked (during seasons now, and in every world from Saturday, November 21). It stops anyone feeding a second account. Networks that many players share, like mobile carriers, schools or satellite internet, don't count.

What are decisions?

About every 40 minutes an issue lands on your desk — strikes, scandals, disasters, opportunities — with several options and their effects shown. There are 249 different scenarios, and some only appear in certain situations (at war, in debt, under sanctions…).

  • ★ Investments: the costly options pay off for a whole day, for example +5% GDP, −10% research costs, +10% defense, or lasting approval or stability. They show as gold tags, and your active effects are listed under Decisions on the Overview page. A new effect of the same kind replaces the old one.
  • 🎲 Gambles show what you win and what backfires. The stakes are high: success brings a big payoff and a day-long bonus, while failure costs a lot and drags your GDP down 5% for 12 hours.
  • Rewards: every answer earns 20 XP, and clearing every pending decision earns one tax collection, +1 approval and 15 bonus XP.
Can the globe spin on its own?

Yes. On the World page, tick Slowly spin the globe under the globe. It turns gently (about once every two and a half minutes). Click or tap the globe to stop it; it starts again a few seconds later. It also pauses while a province is open, during strikes and camera flights, and while the page is hidden. Your choice is remembered on each device.

How do I get more land?

On the World page, use Expand borders to settle an unclaimed province next to your territory (dashed outlines on the map); with warships you can settle across a narrow sea. You can also settle any unclaimed province anywhere: tap it on the map and choose Settle. Land away from your borders costs 1.5× the usual price plus another 1× for every 2,500 km. There's no limit on how many provinces you can hold: each new one just costs a bit more to settle. You can also take land in war, even far from your borders.

Economy

Where can I see what my nation earns and spends?

Open Economy. The first panel, Money in and out, lists everything that entered and left your treasury, biggest first, and names your biggest drain. Use the buttons to switch between Today, Yesterday and the Last 7 days (days change at midnight UTC).

  • Money in: taxes you collected, automatic tax income, province income, market sales, trade routes, aid and deals, casino winnings, rewards.
  • Money out: your budget split into military, health, education and infrastructure; the cost of being at war; unit and Golden Dome upkeep; interest; recruiting; attacks; research; development; market purchases; casino losses; and the cost of holding more land than you can administer.

Only you can see your own numbers. Costs that turned into debt because the treasury was empty are not listed.

How does the economy work?

Your GDP grows with population, technology, infrastructure, education, health, trade and stability, and shrinks with unemployment, inflation, war and sanctions. Tax revenue is a share of GDP: part of it arrives automatically every 10 minutes (roughly enough to cover normal government spending), and the Collect Taxes operation brings in the rest — so the more often you collect, the richer you get. One game year passes every real day.

Can I pay off my national debt?

Yes. On the Economy page, type an amount next to bonds and press Repay debt (leave it empty to repay as much as your treasury allows). Borrowing isn't bad: issuing bonds to expand early is a strong strategy as long as you keep collecting taxes. Interest is charged every 10 minutes, and the rate rises as debt grows past 40% of GDP. You can borrow up to 120% of GDP.

Avoid two things: letting the treasury go deeply negative (below −20% of GDP your government goes bankrupt — debt is cut, but approval, stability and GDP crash and no one lends to you for 48 hours), and debt above twice your GDP, which also triggers a default.

What does the budget do?

On the Economy page you choose total spending (5–40% of GDP) and how it's split:

  • Military pays troop, ship and aircraft upkeep and keeps morale up. Underfund it and morale falls and soldiers desert.
  • Health raises population growth and approval.
  • Education raises GDP and speeds research.
  • Infrastructure raises GDP and lowers unemployment.

Spending is paid from your treasury every 10 minutes.

What about taxes?

Set the rate (5–50%) on the Government page. Rates above 20% cost approval each time you collect and over time. A raise takes 30 minutes to reach your collections (a cut counts at once), so the rate can't be spiked for a single collection; higher taxes also cool inflation.

Very high taxes backfire. Above 25%, every extra point cuts your GDP 1.5% (at 50% that's −37.5%), so you collect less, not more. And if your approval falls under 25 while your rate is above 25%, your stability drops toward 40 + twice your approval no matter how strong your fortifications are, and low stability brings rebellions. Lower taxes give you more approval but less money. Around 20% is the sweet spot for most nations.

Can one player run away with the game?

The game is built so the top stays catchable:

  • Diminishing returns: technology, infrastructure and fortifications give their full boost early on, but each further level adds a little less — and costs a little more.
  • Catch-up research: technology that other nations already know is up to 50% cheaper to research.
  • Development aid: nations far behind the world leader earn up to +50% tax revenue, shrinking as they grow. You'll see it on your Economy page.
  • Army limits: your army can't exceed 4% of your population, and a big army needs a big military budget — underfunded troops lose morale and desert.
Can I borrow money? Can my nation go bankrupt?

Yes and yes. Issue government bonds on the Economy page, up to 120% of your GDP. Interest is charged every 10 minutes and rises as your debt grows; your credit rating runs from AAA down to D.

If debt passes 200% of GDP, or your treasury falls deep into the red, your nation defaults: most of the debt is written off, but the economy crashes, stability and approval collapse, inflation spikes and lenders refuse you for 48 hours.

How do trade and the market work?

Import and export food, oil, steel, goods and munitions on the world market. Prices move with what every nation buys and sells over the last 6 hours, and with world events. Use the quick buttons (50, 250, 1K, 5K, Max buy, Max sell) to set an amount in one tap; the preview shows the exact total and the average price per unit before you trade. Big orders move the price as they fill, so very large orders cost more (or earn less) per unit. Max buy picks the most you can import at a fair price while keeping 15% of your treasury; Max sell picks the most you can export before the price drops much. The market gets deeper as the world grows, and no single nation can push a price beyond about double or half on its own. Trade agreements (up to 5, both sides must agree) give both nations +5% revenue, higher GDP and better prices.

Can the game restock for me while I'm away?

Yes. On the Economy page, under Auto-buy orders, pick a resource, the level to buy at, and how much to buy each time (for example: when oil falls below 2,000, buy 5,000). The game checks every 10 minutes, even when you're offline, and buys on the world market at the normal price.

Orders only go through when you can pay in full from your treasury (they never borrow), and never above your optional price limit. They pause during embargoes, blockades and rogue status. Munitions only fill up to your free depot space. You get a notification if an order can't go through, and you can turn an order off at any time.

What happens if I run out of resources?

No food shrinks your population. No oil weakens your military by 40%. No goods erodes approval. Keep an eye on the Net column on your Overview.

How much can I store? Does food go off?

Food, oil, steel and goods only fit in your warehouses. They hold 48 hours of whichever is bigger, what you make or what you use, plus 24 hours for each warehouse level on the Development page (a full week at level 5). Small nations always get a minimum that grows with their land.

  • Production that doesn't fit is sold automatically on the world market at 60% of the price. The money goes to your treasury.
  • You can't buy more than fits. The Economy page shows how much space you have left.
  • If you already had more than fits when caps arrived, nothing is taken at once: 2% of the extra is sold each hour until it fits.
  • Food spoils if you hoard it. Anything beyond two days of what your people eat loses 0.5% of the extra each hour, and every warehouse level slows that by 10%. Two days' worth never spoils.

Goods and munitions have no cap (munitions still have their depot limit). The Overview page shows each cap next to your stockpile.

Do warships and aircraft cost steel?

Yes. Every 10 minutes each warship uses 0.2 steel, each aircraft 0.1 and each tank 0.02 for spare parts. It shows under Consumption on the Overview page. If your steel runs out, about 1% of your warships, aircraft and tanks fall into disrepair each hour until you have steel again, so keep some in stock or set up an auto-buy order.

Can I stand down or disband units?

Yes, on the Military page under Stand down and disband. Stood-down troops, warships, aircraft and tanks use no oil, steel or upkeep, but they don't fight or defend; bringing them back takes 30 minutes. It's a good way to stretch your oil overnight. Disbanding is permanent, and warships, aircraft and tanks return a quarter of their steel as scrap. Stood-down troops still count toward your army limit.

What happens to my money if I stop playing?

If you don't log in for 7 days or more, money above $1B in your treasury slowly drains away at 2% a day. It stops the moment you log in. You keep your land, army and everything else.

Government, elections & stability

Which form of government should I pick?
  • Democracy: +5% tax revenue, faster research, approval bonus. Elections every 3 days, and wars cost twice the approval.
  • Monarchy: highly stable, small military bonus, no elections.
  • Dictatorship: +15% military strength and war barely dents approval, but lower approval and income.
  • Communist State: recruiting and construction 25% cheaper, lower approval.

You can change at most once every 6 hours; the transition costs 25 stability and 10 approval.

How do elections work?

Democracies vote every 3 days. Your vote share follows approval, unemployment and inflation, plus any campaigning (Election Campaign, up to +12%, opens 24 hours before polling). The Government page shows your projected vote.

  • Lose and a coalition resets your taxes and budget.
  • Lose badly (under 35%) and a new administration also purges ministries.
Can my government be overthrown?

Non-democracies don't hold elections, but if stability and approval both collapse, a revolution can topple the government: it changes form and you lose 30% of your cash and some troops.

War

How do I attack someone?

Tap Attack next to their name — in Diplomacy, on their profile, or on any of their provinces on the map. The war room opens (every attack listed on the left, the live globe on the right) and walks you through it:

  • Rank of Mayor (300 XP) — needed to wage war.
  • Declare war — one tap in the planner. It's public, and ends any alliance or trade deal with that nation.
  • Mobilize — your forces need 10 minutes after the declaration.
  • Pick a target — the planner lists all their provinces, best targets first, with distance, defense, cost and your chance of winning.
  • Choose how many troops and aircraft to send, and tap Launch attack.
Infantry invasion or armored assault?

The ground assault in the war planner has two types:

  • 🪖 Infantry invasion takes the province intact: a city stays a city, and you get the full loot. Use it to absorb a rich province.
  • 🛡 Armored assault needs an Armored Division (Prestige units on the Military page: $5B, 5,000 steel, 3,000 oil, tech 40) and tanks (the Build Tanks operation). Each tank carries 250 troops; only troops with tanks get the bonus. One division gives +40% strength and 60% of the usual losses, two give +60% and 50%, three give +80% and 40%. It burns 1 steel for every 5 troops carried and up to three times the oil, needs a land route (a border or a march), loses some tanks in battle, and wrecks two development levels of what it takes, with half the loot. Use it to break through heavy defences.

Sea, overseas and airborne invasions are always infantry. The game remembers which type you picked last.

Who can I attack?
  • Peace treaties: after making peace you can declare war again straight away, but breaking a treaty less than 24 hours old makes that war cost 3× the usual upkeep for its first hour, and the world is told you broke it.
  • Not players under new-nation protection (48 hours, or 24 hours during a fresh-start season) — they show a blue “Protected” badge with the time left.
  • There is no limit on attacking smaller nations — so choose your wars and your allies carefully.
  • AI nations have no new-nation protection and can be attacked at any time once you are at war.
  • Not a capital — until that nation is down to its capital alone (see Endgame).

You can attack any province of a nation you're at war with — you don't need to border them. Distance matters, though: see “Do I need to border a nation to attack it?”.

How are battles decided?

Your troops (each aircraft counts as 20 soldiers) times technology, training, morale, fuel and government, against the defender's troops plus air support (their air force can add at most as much again as their troops, so planes alone can't hold a nation), fortifications, rank, technology, training, morale, terrain, allies' support, how spread out their empire is (past 10 provinces each province defends a little less: about 72% at 30 provinces, 58% at 60 and 50% at 100, but the capital keeps full strength) and how long they've held the land (+5% per day, up to +50%). There's a little luck either way.

Every attack costs $25, 0.1 oil and 0.05 food per soldier (more for distant targets), lowers approval and stability, adds aggression, and your forces regroup for 30–60 minutes (defense −25%).

Can I attack a nation on the other side of the world?

Yes. Over land, your army marches there (weaker and costlier with distance). Across an ocean, you need a coastal province and at least 5 warships — each carries 75 troops — and your fleet must beat theirs at sea before you land. No fleet or no coast? With tech 4 and 20+ aircraft you can launch an airborne invasion instead: each aircraft flies in 10 troops, at 40% less strength. Long-distance attacks are hard: build up first, or take a nearby province to use as a foothold.

What happens if I'm attacked and lose?
  • The province becomes theirs, with everything it produced.
  • You lose soldiers (up to about a quarter of your army in a single battle) and possibly aircraft.
  • They seize up to 5% of your treasury.
  • Approval −4, stability −3, and the world hears about it.

Your other provinces, technology, buildings and fortifications are untouched. If you win the defense, you keep the land and the attacker loses far more.

What are generals?

Every victory has a 25% chance to reveal a talented officer — up to three serve at once. There are three kinds:

  • Field marshal: +4% attack strength per star.
  • Defense commander: +4% defense in every province per star.
  • Quartermaster general: each star cuts the distance penalty and cost of long-range attacks by 10%.

Field marshals gain a star for every 3 victories (up to 5). A field marshal can be killed when an attack fails, and a defense commander when a province falls. See yours on the Military page.

What are air and missile strikes?

From the war room, you can damage an enemy without invading: hit their military, stockpiles or infrastructure.

  • Air strikes use at least 5 aircraft and 3 oil each. Their air force can shoot some down.
  • Missiles need tech level 25 and cost $400,000, 40 steel, 40 oil and 10 munitions each. You can fire up to 5 per strike at first. That rises by 1 for every 2 tech levels above 25, and by 1 more each at Senator, President and Emperor rank, up to 10. Bigger nations fire bigger salvos: every 4 provinces you hold adds one more missile, up to 20 in all. Fortifications reduce hits, and a Golden Dome shoots most of them down.
  • Dirty bombs (see below) are a third option once you reach tech level 120.

Bigger nations also build more: every 5 provinces adds an extra aircraft to each Air Force order, and every 6 provinces an extra warship to each Navy order.

A strike adds 11 aggression against a real player (3 against an AI nation) when you start the fight, less when you started the war but they are hitting back, and none at all when you are defending yourself or an ally. Strike forces need 30 minutes to rearm.

Every strike is shown on the globe for all players: aircraft fly in formation from the attacker's capital (missiles as fast streaks), with flashes on impact and smoke over the province for about 10 minutes. Everyone gets a short note with a Show me button, and if you are the target your map swings to the strike.

How many provinces can I hold? (Administration)

Every province costs administration: core land 1 (land you've held for 7 days or more), a colony 2 (newer land), and +1 for land more than 3,000 km from your capital. Your capital is free. Your capacity is 20 + 5 per rank + 4 per level of Administrative offices (build up to 5 levels on the Government page). AI nations get +6.

  • Settling needs spare capacity (a new colony takes 2). Conquest always works.
  • Over capacity, production and taxes fall 3% per point over (up to −45%), stability slips (never below 30 from this alone), and there's upkeep every 10 minutes (never more than 2% of your treasury, so it can't bankrupt you).
  • Badly over (25% or more), you get a 24-hour warning, then your farthest colonies slowly start to break away (roughly one an hour at most) and become free land.
  • A new nation founded right next to an over-capacity empire may be handed up to 2 of its border provinces, so newcomers always have room. It costs the empire nothing, since that land was over its capacity anyway.
  • Land far from your capital (over 3,000 km) can also break away now and then, after its own 24-hour warning. Each development level makes a province 15% less likely to break away (up to 60% at Metropolis), and the least developed far province goes first. No province ever becomes rebellion-proof.

Release land any time with no penalty, build offices, rank up, or simply wait: colonies become cheaper core land after 7 days.

What are vassals?

When you win a siege of an enemy capital you choose: conquer it (the nation falls, you take its capital, a quarter of its treasury and half its stockpiles, and its other land becomes free) or make it your vassal. A vassal keeps its land, you take 10% of its treasury straight away, and it pays you tribute every 10 minutes: half a tax collection and 10% of its production. You can't attack each other. After 48 hours a vassal may declare independence, which means war. You can release a vassal any time. Vassals' land doesn't count against your administration, so they're a way to grow rich without overstretching.

Why are some provinces harder to take?

The longer you hold a province and the more you invest in it, the better it defends: +5% defense for every day held (up to +100% after 20 days) and +15% for every development level. Tap a province on the World map to see its holding bonus.

Is there room for new players?

Always. A new nation starts on free land at least two provinces from anyone; if the map is crowded the rule relaxes, and if every province is taken, a nation gives up a province for the newcomer (never its capital): first the most overstretched nation, which loses no output, and if no one is overstretched, the biggest nation gives up one of its least-developed outer provinces. A province's size on the map doesn't matter: every province counts the same, and what makes one valuable is its type, development and coast.

What are heavy weapons?

Serious options short of a nuke, listed in the war room by tier. Each has its own cooldown, separate from air and missile strikes.

Weapons come in four tiers, and each tier takes far more research than the last, so the biggest weapons stay rare and special:

  • Tier 1 · Tactical (tech 10–40): artillery 10, naval bombardment 20, drone swarm 30, naval blockade 40. Missile strikes need tech 25.
  • Tier 2 · Strategic (tech 70–120): cruise missile 70, carpet bombing 80, cyber attack 90, special forces raid 100, submarine strike 110, dirty bomb 120.
  • Tier 3 · Advanced (tech 150–200): ballistic missile 150, stealth bomber run 170, submarine missile salvo 180, carrier strike group 200. The prestige units they need unlock at tech 120 (stealth bomber wing), 140 (nuclear submarine) and 160 (supercarrier).
  • Tier 4 · Superweapons (tech 250–320): hypersonic missile 250, EMP strike 280, orbital strike 320.
  • Beyond the tiers: the Golden Dome needs tech 300, a nuclear deterrent tech 350 and a launchable warhead tech 400.

What each weapon does:

  • 🚀 Ballistic missile (tier 3, tech 150, 120 munitions, Governor): one heavy warhead on a random province. It kills 4% of the population and 10% of the army, destroys a level of infrastructure and cuts GDP 5%. A Golden Dome stops only about half as many of these as cruise missiles. One every 6 hours.
  • ⚓ Naval bombardment (tier 1, tech 20, 40 munitions, Mayor): your fleet (10+ warships) shells one of their coastal provinces, killing troops and sinking ships. Their navy shoots back. Costs 5 oil per warship. One every 3 hours.
  • 💻 Cyber attack (tier 2, tech 90, Mayor): their tax revenue drops 25% and research costs rise 50% for 12 hours. No one dies. Only a 35% chance it's traced to you; if not, it adds no aggression and they never learn who did it. One every 8 hours.
  • 🛸 Drone swarm (tier 1, tech 30, 30 munitions, Mayor): kills up to 6% of their army and some aircraft. A Golden Dome can't stop it, but their air force shoots part of the swarm down. Your own air force escorts it, and at least 40% always gets through. One every 2 hours.
  • ⛴ Naval blockade (tier 1, tech 40, 40 munitions, Mayor, 20+ warships): for 12 hours their tax revenue drops 30%, GDP 5%, and they can't trade on the world market. One every 12 hours.
  • 🎯 Special forces raid (tier 2, tech 100, 25 munitions, Governor, $15M): steals 4% of their treasury (up to $100M) and 10% of their biggest stockpile, and wrecks a development level in their most developed province. If it fails, your commandos are captured and they get a 24-hour just cause. One every 8 hours.
  • ☄ Hypersonic missile (tier 4, tech 250, 200 munitions, Prime Minister, $250M): kills 6% of their people and 15% of their army, infrastructure −2, GDP −7%. No Golden Dome can intercept it. One every 12 hours.
  • 🌊 Submarine missile salvo (tier 3, tech 180, needs a Nuclear Submarine, $300M, 150 munitions): your sub surfaces off their coast and fires 3 missiles plus 1 per submarine you own. A Golden Dome stops some.
  • 🛩 Stealth bomber run (tier 3, tech 170, needs a Stealth Bomber Wing, $400M, 120 munitions): one bomber crosses the map slowly and unseen and drops a bomb that shakes the screen. No air defence or dome can stop it.
  • 🚢 Carrier strike group (tier 3, tech 200, needs a Supercarrier, $500M, 150 munitions): the fleet sails to their coast, its air wing strikes their troops, ships and aircraft, then it sails home.
  • 🛰 Orbital strike (tier 4, tech 320, 400 munitions, Sovereign, $750M): a precision strike on their capital: 10% of their people and 25% of their army killed, infrastructure −3, the capital loses a development level, GDP −12%. No shield can stop it and there is no fallout. One every 48 hours.
  • ⚡ EMP strike (tier 4, tech 280, Sovereign, $1B, 5,000 steel, 5,000 oil, 4,000 goods, 500 munitions): knocks out 30% of their aircraft, with tax revenue −40% and GDP −8% for 12 hours. It can take their Golden Dome offline (it turns electric blue on the globe), but stronger domes resist: a level 1 dome always goes down for 12 hours, a level 5 dome only 60% of the time and for just 4 hours. No fallout. One every 36 hours during a season.
  • 💥 Artillery barrage (tier 1, tech 10, Elder, $3M, 20 munitions): shells their front line, killing about 3.5% of their army (less against heavy fortifications) and knocking their fortifications down a level. One every 2 hours.
  • 🔥 Cruise missile (tier 2, tech 70, 50 munitions, Mayor, $15M): a precision strike on their most developed province: destroys a development level, a level of infrastructure and 3% of their army. A Golden Dome can shoot it down. One every 4 hours.
  • ✈ Carpet bombing (tier 2, tech 80, 60 munitions, Governor, 40+ aircraft): up to 200 of your bombers hit a province, killing up to 2.5% of the people and 7% of the army and destroying infrastructure. Their air force shoots some bombers down. Costs 4 oil per bomber. One every 6 hours.
  • 🌊 Submarine strike (tier 2, tech 110, 60 munitions, Governor, 5+ warships, $10M): torpedoes about 20% of their warships (more if your tech is better). You may lose a submarine. One every 6 hours.

Each of these looks different on the globe: shells from your nearest border for artillery, warships firing from the sea, helicopters for a raid, a beam from a satellite for the orbital strike, a blue electric pulse for an EMP, torpedo wakes for submarines, and a fleet that stays off the coast for as long as a blockade lasts. Cyber attacks are invisible.

What are munitions?

Every missile, heavy weapon and dirty bomb uses munitions from your stockpile: 10 per missile, from 20 (artillery) up to 400 (orbital strike) for heavy weapons, and 100 for a dirty bomb. Cyber attacks, air strikes, invasions and nuclear missiles don't use them.

Make them on the Military page: each costs $300, 2 steel and 1 oil. Your factories can make 200 an hour plus 10 for every tech level, and your depots hold 1,000 plus 40 for every tech level. You can also buy and sell munitions on the world market (only up to your free depot space) and on the black market. Stock up before a war: when you run out, you can't strike until you make or buy more. Every nation started with 300.

Why does research get so expensive at high tech?

Research (Operations page) gets a little pricier every level. Above tech 200 it climbs much faster: each level costs 3% more than the one before, on top of the normal rise. A level costs about 4 times the old price at tech 250, 19 times at 300 and 84 times at 350. That keeps the higher weapon tiers, the Golden Dome and nuclear weapons a real long-term goal that only sustained hard work reaches. Nations behind the world's tech leader still research cheaper, so they can catch up.

Does a higher tech level protect me?

Yes, a lot. The further an attacker's tech is behind yours, the less their strikes, heavy weapons and dirty bombs do to you, and the weaker their invasions are. A tech-30 nation hitting a tech-220 nation does about 42% of the normal damage, and its armies fight at about 65% strength. It works the other way too: a far more advanced attacker does up to 1.5× damage. Against a much more advanced nation, more of your missiles are shot down, and a submarine strike may not sink anything. The war room shows the tech gap before you attack.

Nuclear missiles are the exception: they are devastating whatever your tech.

What spy operations are there?

On the Intelligence page, your agents can run one operation every 20 minutes. Besides stealing technology, sabotage, unrest, election interference, rebellion, fake news and siphoning funds, there are:

  • 🛰 Satellite reconnaissance (1 agent): a full intelligence report on a nation's treasury, army, fleet, air force, agents, tech, generals and weapons.
  • ⚡ Sabotage the power grid (3 agents): production −40% and GDP −3% for 8 hours.
  • 📉 Crash their stock market (3 agents): GDP −4% at once and tax revenue −20% for 8 hours.
  • 🕵 Turn a double agent (2 agents): they lose 2 agents and you gain 1.
  • ☣ Contaminate food supplies (2 agents): spoils a third of their food. Health −6.
  • Advanced operations are tiered like the heavy weapons: each needs research and rank, and its price grows with your own income (the prices here are the minimum).
  • 🏦 Leak to their bankers (Tier 2: tech 90, Senator, 5 agents, from $30M): their credit rating drops one step for 24 hours.
  • 📡 Wiretap their alliance (Tier 3: tech 150, Prime Minister, 6 agents, from $60M): listen to their alliance chat for 1 hour on your Intelligence page. The line is poor: you catch about half of what they say after the bug is planted, with some words missing. Half the time they are warned that the chat is bugged.
  • 🎭 False flag (Tier 3: tech 180, Prime Minister, 8 agents, from $100M): frame a nation. If your next operation in the next 6 hours is caught, it is blamed on them instead of you, but 1 time in 3 the frame-up is exposed and the whole world hears about it.

Spies are anonymous. When an operation works, the target never learns who did it. When it fails you lose some agents, and about 1 time in 5 they are identified. Only then does the target learn it was you; your aggression rises and an alliance between you breaks. Only a caught armed rebellion (24 hours) gives the target a just cause to attack you. Small operations like unrest, fake news or sabotage never do. The rest of the time the target only hears that a foreign plot was foiled. Only identified armed rebellions make the public news.

What happens if the anti-cheat system flags me?

Scripts, macros and auto-clickers are not allowed. The system works in steps, and each step only happens if the signs continue after the one before:

  • Warning: a private message listing what it saw. Nothing is done to your account.
  • 24-hour suspension if the signs continue 12 hours or more after the warning, judged only on what you did after it.
  • 72-hour suspension the second time within 14 days.
  • Locked until an admin reviews it the third time, or straight away on hard proof (for example a human check answered by a program).

While you are suspended your nation is protected from attack and nothing is taken. You can always message the Helpdesk to appeal; if an admin marks you as not cheating, the automatic steps leave you alone for 7 days.

What are supply lines?

Provinces far from home are harder to defend. Each province's distance is measured to your nearest base: your capital, or an outpost (whose reach grows with its level).

  • Within 2,000 km: full defense.
  • 2,000 to 3,500 km: two-thirds defense.
  • Beyond 3,500 km: one-third defense.

Your capital and the Rising Power are never affected. The Territories page and each province's panel show its supply. Outposts and outpost upgrades extend your reach. Admins can switch supply lines off.

What are outposts?

From the rank of Mayor you can have up to 4 outposts. Tap a province on the globe and either turn one of your own provinces into an outpost ($25M, 500 steel, 300 oil) or establish one on unclaimed land anywhere in the world ($60M, 1,000 steel, 600 oil).

  • Staging ground: attacks on targets within 2,500 km of an outpost launch from it, with no distance penalty or extra cost.
  • Defense: the outpost and your provinces next to it defend 10% better (15% at level 2, 20% at level 3).
  • Supply base: provinces near an outpost count as close to home for supply lines (see below).
  • Levels: upgrade an outpost to level 2 (rank of Governor) and level 3 (rank of Senator) from its province panel. Each level reaches further for supply lines (+500 km, then +1,000 km) and defends better. Upgrades cost more for bigger economies.
  • An outpost always costs just 1 administration and never breaks away from overstretch or distance.
  • If the province is conquered, the outpost is lost. You can dismantle one any time to build elsewhere (one new outpost every 2 hours).
What are seasons and the Hall of Fame?

Every so often the game runs a season, a competition for a set time. There are two kinds:

  • Fresh-start seasons: everyone founds a brand-new nation in an empty world on the same day, so newcomers and veterans have exactly the same chance. Nations are ranked by final score. Your real nation is saved and set aside, untouched, until the season ends (see below).
  • Normal seasons: played on the current map. Nations are ranked by what they achieve during the season, such as score gained, provinces gained, GDP growth, battles won or relief sent.

Live standings are at the top of the Rankings page. Only battles won against a real opponent (at least half your military strength, at most 3 per nation per day) count for a season, and relief only counts up to what each disaster actually needs.

When a season ends, the top three win the prizes announced for it and a title on their profile, and extra titles can go to the leaders in other categories. The final standings are written into the Hall of Fame permanently: once recorded, nobody can change or remove them, not even the administrators.

When a new season is coming, a countdown banner at the top of the screen shows exactly how long is left. Tap ✕ to hide it.

Rewards: the top 10 nations win cash and XP. The top 3 get a permanent title and a 🏆, 🥈 or 🥉 badge; 4th to 10th get a 🎖 top-10 badge; the leader in each extra award gets a title like Warlord of Season 1, a 🏅 badge and any cash and XP set for that award; and everyone who plays during the season earns a 🎗 participation badge. Badges show on your profile forever.

Champion rewards: every season champion unlocks a gold frame for their flag (Government page, Flag & colours) and two map colours no one else can use: Champion gold and Platinum.

Can I reach nuclear weapons and the other late-game things in a 7-day season?

Yes, if you play actively. Inside a season every tech requirement is a tenth of the usual: missiles at tech 3, armored divisions at tech 4, dirty bombs at tech 12, supercarriers at tech 16, the Golden Dome at tech 30, a nuclear program at tech 35 and warheads at tech 40. The ranks needed are a step lower as well (Prime Minister for a nuclear program, President for warheads).

The big-ticket items also cost a fraction of their usual price: nuclear programs, warheads, the Golden Dome, heavy weapons, elite units, grand projects, world wonders and the Space Race. A nuclear program must run for 6 hours before you can build a warhead, and the warhead takes 6 hours to assemble. Someone who plays most of each day can have a warhead around day 5; you will need to buy steel, oil and goods on the market along the way.

Rogue status is lighter too. In a season, attacking a player adds 15 aggression instead of 20, rogue status lasts at least 6 hours instead of 12, a rogue state can still buy and sell on the world market (at the worse prices sanctioned nations pay), and a nuclear strike makes you a rogue state for 24 hours.

None of this applies to the real world, which keeps its normal requirements and prices.

What happens to my real nation during a fresh-start season?

Nothing at all. When the season starts, the whole real world (every nation, alliance, treaty, war, World Council vote and market listing) is saved and set aside. Nobody can attack it, tax it or touch it while the season runs.

When the season ends on its announced date, the real world comes back exactly as it was saved, and your prizes (cash, XP, titles, badges and champion rewards) are added to your real nation. If you didn't have a real nation yet, your prizes wait for you and arrive when you found one.

One thing does keep going: timers like sanctions, protection shields and cooldowns run in real time, so they may have finished by the time the real world returns. Your season nation is only for the season: it is put away when the real world comes back.

Everything you bought for your real nation (cosmetics like chat colours and flag frames, World Wonders, grand projects, prestige units) is saved with it. Your season nation starts without them, and they are all back when the season ends. Poker chips at a table when the season starts go back to your real treasury first.

Season rules: new-nation protection lasts 24 hours instead of 48, the Rising Power can't appear during the first 2 days, and you can't send aid, trade offers or relief to (or buy black-market goods from) an account on the same network as yours, so nobody can feed a second account.

Nukes in the season: they come earlier and everything about them is timed for a 7-day week. A nuclear program needs tech 350 and a launchable warhead tech 400 (after the program has run 12 hours, plus 12 hours to build). You can launch it at any nation you are at war with as soon as it is armed; there is no waiting period. The Golden Dome needs tech 300 (upgrades at 320 to 400), so a defence comes before a nuke. A launch makes you a rogue state for 36 hours, the victim is crippled for 36 hours, fallout lasts 24 hours, the nuclear winter 18 hours, and every nation hit has just cause against you for 3 days. Each nation can still launch only once. Running costs scale with season wealth: $1.5M every 10 minutes, and inspection fees of up to $1B a day.

Season timers: everything that normally takes days is sized for a 7-day week. The Golden Dome builds in 12 hours (upgrades in 3 to 6), the orbital strike recharges in 24 hours and the EMP in 12, you can move your capital every 24 hours (after holding the province for 12), vassals can declare independence after 24 hours, trade routes reach full strength after 2 days, elections come every 48 hours, a nation down to its capital has 12 hours before it can be besieged, and alliance invites last 24 hours.

No province limit: inside a fresh-start season world there is no administration limit, so you can hold as many provinces as you can settle or conquer, with no breakaways. The usual limit applies again when the real world returns.

What is a Rapid redeployment?

Every so often, while your attack timers are counting down, a stroke of luck clears them: a defecting enemy general, an overnight railway, a forgotten ammunition depot, and so on. Army regrouping, attacks, air and missile strikes, spy operations and the faster heavy weapons are ready again at once. It can happen at most once every 12 hours. The EMP, orbital strike and nuclear weapons are never reset.

What is the World Casino?

In the menu, below Alliance. Play Blackjack, Roulette, Slots (four machines) and High-Low dice with your nation's treasury. In blackjack you can double down on your first two cards, or split a pair (two cards of the same value) into two hands with a second bet of the same size; split aces get one card each, and 21 on a split hand pays like a normal win. Bets run from $10,000 up to $1 billion on every game (or whatever is in your treasury, if that is less). Like a real casino, the house keeps a small edge (it returns about 93–97% of what's bet over time), so play for fun. All the dice, cards and spins are provably fair: the casino shows a fingerprint of its secret seed before you bet, and you can reveal it any time and recompute every result on the Fair play page. Big wins are announced to the world. Every table shows its all-time record, so you can see whether the house or the players are ahead overall.

What is the slots jackpot?

One pot shared by every slot machine and every player. 1% of every slot stake is added to it, and the running total is shown at the top of the casino. Any spin on any machine can win the whole pot, on top of what the spin itself pays. Your chance on each spin is your stake divided by $5 trillion (so $1B gives 1 in 5,000, and the $5B limit gives 1 in 1,000); on average it is won once for every $5 trillion wagered, so it builds up to around $50B before someone wins it. After a win it starts again at $1B. The jackpot draw uses the same provably fair number stream as the spin, taken after the spin's own numbers.

What are world records?

Fourteen titles that only one nation holds at a time (Prestige → Achievements & records). The holder wears a badge on their nation. Beat the holder's number and the record passes to you; they get a note, and so do you. Records cover the casino (most wagered, biggest winnings, biggest losses, biggest single win, poker winnings and losses), war (battles won, capitals captured) and the world (expeditions, trades, aid given, tax collections, daily missions, prestige). They are checked every minute.

What is the Mystery Box auction?

Every 2 days a prize box goes up for auction for 24 hours (Prestige → World races). The highest bid wins. Outbid bidders get their money back at once; the winning bid is destroyed.

  • 🎰 Casino Vault: 50–150 free spins on any slot, each staked at half an hour of your tax income (at least $100M, at most $5B). The house pays the stake; every win is yours.
  • 🪖 Arsenal Crate: soldiers up to 30% of your army limit, 10–20% more warships and aircraft, tanks, 2,000–6,000 munitions, +10 training and +15 morale.
  • 📜 Treasury Bond: 60–100 hours of your tax income, a fifth at once and the rest over 4 days.
  • 🌾 Golden Harvest: 36–60 hours of your food, oil, steel and goods production at once.
  • 🎓 Royal Academy: +3 to +5 tech levels.
  • 🕵 Spymaster Kit: 60–100% more spies (at least 60), +5 training, and your covert cooldown cleared.
  • 🕊 Diplomat's Pardon: lifts every World Council measure, returns frozen assets, clears aggression, +15 approval and stability (not nuclear rogue status).
  • 👑 Prestige Chest: +25 to +40 prestige and a rare badge.

Prizes are rolled when the box is won and sized to the winner's economy. 1 box in 8 is lucky: everything inside is doubled.

What is the tables jackpot?

A second shared pot for every casino game except slots: blackjack, roulette, baccarat, craps, high-low dice, video poker and crash. 1% of every stake on those games is added to it, and it is shown next to the slots jackpot at the top of the casino. Any settled bet can win the whole pot on top of its own result, win or lose. The chance is the same as for slots: your stake divided by $5 trillion ($1B gives 1 in 5,000, and it never goes better than 1 in 500). It starts again at $1B after a win. Poker is not part of it, because poker is played against other players. The draw for this jackpot is made by the server when the bet is settled; it is separate from the provably fair numbers that decide the game itself, so those can still be checked exactly as before.

How does the weekly slots tournament work?

Every week (Monday to Monday, UTC) the three players with the best single win multiplier on any slot machine share a prize pool: 60% / 30% / 10%. The pool starts at $1B and grows by 0.5% of every slot stake. A spin counts if its stake is $1M or more, and only your best spin of the week is kept, so a small nation has the same chance as a big one. The board, the pool and the time left are at the top of the casino. Prizes are paid automatically when the week ends; if nobody entered, the pool carries over.

What are the big-win celebrations and the win feed?

A slot win of 20× your stake or more gets a Big win banner with a coin shower (50×: Mega win, 100×: Epic win). Click or tap to close it. The Big wins list at the top of the casino shows recent wins of 5× or more by any nation, on any game.

Who are the house players at the poker tables?

Computer players that take the empty seats when you sit down alone, so there is always a game. They are marked (house), play with the house's chips, and leave as soon as no player is seated. When another nation sits down, they make room. Players-only tables never have house players.

How does poker work?

Want real people only? Anyone can start a players-only table from the poker room: pick the stakes and your buy-in, and a hand begins as soon as a second player sits down. The table closes by itself after 10 minutes with nobody seated, and you can have one open at a time.

Open World Casino → Poker to play no-limit Texas Hold'em against other nations. There are six tables at three stakes: Embassy (blinds $50K/$100K, buy-in $2M–$10M), Treasury (blinds $500K/$1M, buy-in $20M–$100M) Imperial (blinds $5M/$10M, buy-in $200M–$1B) and Sovereign (blinds $25M/$50M, buy-in $1B–$5B). Your buy-in comes out of your treasury and your chips go back the moment you leave the table.

  • You have 25 seconds to act. If time runs out the game checks for you, or folds if there is a bet to call. Miss two turns and you are sat out; after 10 minutes away you are stood up and paid out.
  • The house keeps 2% of pots that reach the flop, never more than two big blinds. Hands that end before the flop are free.
  • You can play at one table at a time, and friends on the same connection cannot sit at the same table.
  • Cards are shuffled by the server with a cryptographic random generator, and nobody can see another player's cards until a showdown.
  • If a season starts or the world is rolled back, every table is cashed out first, so no chips are ever lost.
What is a dirty bomb?

A missile carrying radioactive material: the weapon you can use long before nukes. It needs tech level 120 and the rank of Governor, and each bomb costs 100 munitions, $15,000,000, 500 steel, 500 oil and 300 goods. Fire it from the war room's Strike section (one every 12 hours).

It hits a random province of the enemy: about 6% of their troops and 2% of their people are lost, 10% of their food is contaminated, and a green radioactive cloud hangs over the province for 24 hours, visible to everyone on the globe. While it lasts, the nation's GDP is 6% lower, and its people slowly sicken and lose stability. A Golden Dome can shoot it down.

It adds 33 aggression against a real player (8 against an AI nation, none when defending yourself) and makes you a likely target for a World Council condemnation.

What is a nuclear deterrent?

A very expensive project (tech level 350, rank of President, and a base price of $500,000,000, 4,000 steel, 4,000 oil and 2,500 goods that rises by half, ×1.5, for every nation that already has a deterrent) found on the Military page. While you have one, your capital defends twice as hard and its last stand lasts twice as long, so it is very hard to take. And if it falls anyway, the dead hand fires your last nuclear missile at the conqueror's capital (their Golden Dome may stop it). A rogue state loses the extra defence. The whole world is alerted, your aggression jumps by 40 (likely sanctions), and it costs $300,000 upkeep every 10 minutes.

Mandatory inspections: every nuclear nation is inspected once a day and pays for it automatically: $10,000,000 plus 1% of its GDP (up to $80,000,000), and 50% more while it holds a warhead. If the treasury can't cover it, the rest is added to the national debt.

What is the Golden Dome?

The ultimate missile shield: a golden dome raised over the heart of your nation, visible on everyone's globe. Missiles it stops blow up on the dome itself and never reach your airspace. It shoots down about 75% of incoming missiles and has a 40% chance to intercept a nuclear missile before it detonates. Air strikes are not affected.

It's meant to be rare. It needs tech level 300 and the rank of Prime Minister, and its price rises by half (×1.5) for every nation that already has a dome, so the first few are the cheapest, and costs $750,000,000, 12,000 steel, 9,000 goods and 6,000 oil. It takes 48 hours to build. Running it costs $1,350,000 every 10 minutes (about $8.1M an hour), paid straight from your treasury, and half as much again for every level above 1. If your treasury goes negative, the dome goes offline until you are back above $0.

An intercepted nuclear missile does not detonate, but the attacker's warhead is spent and the launch still makes it a rogue state.

What is the green circle on the globe?

It marks ground zero of a nuclear strike: a radioactive zone that stays on everyone's globe for as long as the target is in ruins (48 hours), fading as it recovers. For the first 24 hours a dashed outer ring shows how far the fallout reaches.

What is a nuclear test?

A nation with a nuclear deterrent but no warhead can detonate a device in the open ocean to show the world what it has. The tester chooses to test now or in 5, 10 or 15 minutes. Every nation is warned straight away (in chat, by notification, and with a countdown bar on screen), then everyone online sees a 30-second countdown and the blast on the globe: a flash, a column of water, a mushroom cloud and shockwaves across the sea. A steam cloud lingers over the site for about ten minutes.

It costs $5,000,000 and can be done once a week (from the Military page). It is not free of consequences: the tester's aggression rises to at least 70, which means international sanctions, one step from rogue status. Tests are banned while the Non-proliferation Treaty is in force.

Can nukes actually be launched?

Yes, but only as a last resort, and the whole world will know. A nation with a deterrent that has been operational for 24 hours, tech level 400, the rank of Sovereign, $500,000,000, 9,000 steel, 9,000 oil and 6,000 goods can assemble one launchable warhead. Assembly takes 24 hours and every nation is warned it's coming. Once armed, it can be launched at any nation you are at war with, straight away (or one that nuked you).

  • Ground zero: 45% of the population and 70% of the army killed, half the navy, 60% of aircraft and 55% of stockpiles lost, GDP −60%, and up to 5 infrastructure levels destroyed. The target keeps collecting taxes (fallout still cuts them nearby).
  • The launcher: its own tax collection stops for 72 hours (36 during a season), on top of rogue status. Firing back at a nation that nuked you in the last 72 hours is exempt.
  • Fallout: every nation with land within 2,600 km loses 10% of its population, 20 health and 15 approval, and has its tax revenue halved for 48 hours. Each of them gets a just cause against the launcher for 7 days (attacking it adds no aggression), and AI nations hit by fallout may declare war on the launcher.
  • Nuclear winter: the entire world's tax revenue drops 15% for 36 hours.
  • The launcher becomes a rogue state for at least 72 hours, loses all allies, and can't build another warhead for 14 days.
  • Mutually assured destruction: if the target also has an armed warhead and Fire back automatically if I'm nuked is on, it fires back at the launcher's capital the moment it is hit. That switch is under your armed warhead on the Military page (Nuclear); it is on by default, and only you can see it. Untick it to keep your warhead and decide yourself. Firing back counts as self-defense: it doesn't stop your taxes.

Every player sees the detonation flash on screen and a glowing ☢ on the globe for 72 hours. It's all recoverable: population and the economy rebuild over time. Nukes never capture land or topple a nation by themselves.

How do I see what each province is worth?

Open the Territories page. It lists every province with its development level, what it produces every 10 minutes after all your bonuses, its defense multiplier (development, time held, mountains, outposts, capital, and how thinly your army is spread), its distance from your capital, the administration it uses and its breakaway risk. Filters pick out provinces that are good to build on, at risk, or worth releasing.

Can I sell, trade or give up provinces?

Yes. Tap one of your provinces on the globe:

  • Sell or trade opens a deal with another player. Put up to 3 provinces on each side, together with cash or resources, for example “Bavaria for $40M” or “Bavaria for your Normandy”. Your cash and resources are held until they answer (24 hours); provinces only change hands when the deal is accepted.
  • Release gives a province up. It becomes free land anyone can settle.

The rules:

  • A capital can never be traded or released.
  • The buyer needs spare administration capacity, and land arrives with no troops, so garrison it.
  • A traded province can't change hands again for 24 hours.
  • No land deals between nations at war with each other, or with rogue states or nations under embargo. AI nations don't trade land.
  • A deal that gives land away costs −3 approval. Releasing a province costs nothing, and you can release as many as you like.

Every land deal and release is announced to the world.

Can my empire get too big?

No. There's no limit and no penalty for size: hold as many provinces as you can win or settle. Each new province you settle costs a bit more than the last, and newly conquered land still needs a garrison so it doesn't rebel.

Why did the game ask me to tap a shape or type a code?

That's the quick check. Once in a while, at random, an action asks you to tap one shape in a small picture (such as "tap the heart") or to type a short code shown in a picture. It keeps scripts and auto-clickers out of the game. Active players see it roughly every 30 to 60 minutes; it only takes a few seconds. Codes are not case-sensitive. If the picture is hard to read, press New picture or New code. Three wrong answers in a row pause your actions for 2 minutes. Five wrong answers in a row lock the account for an admin to review.

Accounts that show clear signs of a script or auto-clicker (for example, clicks made by a program instead of a person, or actions sent from outside the game page) are locked automatically for an admin to review. Your nation is safe while it is reviewed. If you think it was a mistake, an admin can release it.

Can conquered provinces rebel?

Yes. For 24 hours after you capture a province it is restless. To hold it, your army needs 800 troops for each newly conquered province plus 200 for each other province you own. The further short you are, the likelier new land rises up. If its former owner still borders it, it returns to them; otherwise it breaks away as free land. The war room and the Military page show exactly how many troops you need.

How do I fight back or protect myself?
  • Counterattack: for 24 hours after someone hits you, the strength protections don't shield them from you.
  • Allies automatically send 10% of their troops to your defense.
  • Fortify (stronger defense with every level), train, build aircraft, and hold land longer.
  • Take it to the World Council for sanctions or an embargo.
What are aggression and sanctions?

Every attack and strike adds aggression, which fades slowly over time.

Aggression punishes starting fights, not defending yourself:

  • Attacking a real player: each ground attack adds 20 and each air or missile strike adds 11.
  • Attacking an AI nation: much lighter: 5 per attack and 3 per strike, and fighting AI nations can never push your aggression past 34, so on its own it can never get you condemned, sanctioned or made a rogue state. Only attacks on real players can take you higher.
  • Fighting the Rising Power adds no aggression: the whole world wants it stopped.
  • Once they hit back, the nation that declared the war still adds a little (7 per attack and 4 per strike against a player, 2 and 1 against an AI). It started the war, so it stays the aggressor.
  • Defending: if a nation declared war on you, or hit you or one of your allies in the last 24 hours (any battle, strike or nuke), fighting back adds no aggression at all, so you can never be sanctioned or made a rogue state for defending yourself. The same goes for wars you joined through a Collective Defense Pact, and for a nuclear second strike: firing back at a nation that nuked you in the last 72 hours (or your armed warhead retaliating automatically) doesn't make you a rogue state.
  • Rogue states: attacking a rogue state adds nothing.

Aggression fades by 6 points an hour, even while you're offline. At 50 aggression you're automatically under international sanctions: worse market prices, no trade-deal benefits and no new trade agreements. At 75 you are declared a rogue state (see below). The World Council can also vote to sanction you, which makes you a rogue state straight away.

Can the admins or other players cheat?

Players can't: every action is checked and applied by the server, so nobody can edit their own money, troops or timers, and suspicious activity is flagged. For the admins, the Fair play record shows a fingerprint of the exact game code running and the date it last changed, the settings in force, and every admin action that affected the game — nation edits, rollbacks, setting changes, events — logged automatically with exactly what changed.

What are national laws?

On the Government page you can pass laws, each with a real trade-off — for example Conscription (50% bigger recruit batches and a higher troop limit, but lower approval), Universal healthcare, Free education, Press censorship, Green energy, Free market, Martial law and Business tax cuts. Passing a law costs 0.5% of your GDP; repealing is free. You can hold 1 law at first and up to 5 as you rank up, and parliament needs 6 hours between changes.

What are megaprojects (wonders of the world)?

There are 13 megaprojects, and each nation can own at most 3, so more nations get one. New ones: World Health Institute, Grand University, Three Rivers Dam, Central Reserve Bank, World Broadcasting Tower, Intercontinental Canal, Hyperloop Network and Ministry of Efficiency (at the bottom of the Operations & Projects page). Wonders taken by conquering a capital are kept, but count toward the 3.

On the Operations & Projects page, nations of Mayor rank and above can build wonders: the Global Stock Exchange, Space Program, Great Wall, Olympic Games and Grand Global Port. Each is built in 3–5 stages, 8 hours apart, and gives a permanent bonus. Only one nation in the world can own each wonder — if a rival finishes first, you get half your money back. Capture a nation's capital and you take its wonders.

What are daily missions and achievements?

Every day you get three missions on the Projects page (collect taxes 8 times, win a battle, reach 70% approval…). The “send aid” mission counts aid worth at least a tenth of what the mission pays (at least $1M), so it rewards real help, not $1 gifts. Each pays cash and 30 XP, and finishing all three pays a bonus. Achievements are permanent badges — First Victory, Conqueror, Empire, Billion-Dollar Economy, Wonder of the World and more — shown on your public nation page. Each one pays a reward the moment you earn it: 100, 250 or 600 XP depending on how hard it is, plus cash (2% of your GDP, at least $2M, doubled or tripled for the harder ones).

Can I trade directly with another nation?

Yes. Open a nation's profile and choose Offer a deal: offer cash or resources in exchange for something you want, with no market fees. What you offer is held until they accept, decline, or 24 hours pass (then it comes back to you). AI nations accept deals that are fair at market prices. You can't deal with nations you're at war with, rogue states, or nations under embargo.

What are expeditions?

From the Projects page (Elder rank and up) you can send one expedition at a time: a mountain survey, desert prospecting, jungle expedition, deep-sea expedition or Arctic research station. The cost grows with your economy (about 0.2% of your GDP for each hour away), so a trip is worth making at any size.

Pick how bold to be. Careful trips rarely go wrong but pay a little. Daring trips pay much more and find more legendary treasure, but about 1 in 5 is lost along with some troops. Every card shows the exact chances before you send it. On average all three pay back more than they cost.

The expedition map. Every destination has its own map. When you send a team, the map opens and shows the route from the starting point to the goal. Open it any time from the Projects page to watch the team move along its path.

Three stops, three decisions. At three places along the way the team runs into something (a storm, a sealed door, a rival crew) and the map pops up to ask for your orders. A bold order is a gamble: if it pays off the haul grows 10%; if it backfires the trip becomes more dangerous (9% more chance of disaster). A careful order has no surprises and trims the danger a little. Some stops also offer a paid order (about 8% of the trip cost) for a small, sure gain. If you don't answer before the next stop, they play it safe and morale drops. There are over 200 different situations, so no two trips are the same.

Crew morale. The crew starts out steady. Every gamble that pays off (or paid help) lifts their morale a step, every one that backfires (or a stop left without orders) drops it a step, from Shaken to Fearless. Each step moves the odds of the next gamble by 5 points, from 55% up to 75%.

Choosing the route. At the first two stops you also pick where the team heads next. Rough going pays 6% more and adds 3% danger, easy going is 2% safer, and steady going changes nothing. The route has to be chosen with your orders, early in the leg. The map shows each place's kind with a symbol (▲ rough, ● easy, ■ steady), and the parts of the map the team has not reached yet stay in the fog.

Logbook. The map opens by itself when a trip comes home, showing what was found. Your last six trips are kept in a logbook on the Projects page, so you can look back at any of them.

Turning back. From the first stop on you can call the team home early. They come back safe with a smaller haul that grows with the distance covered, usually less than the trip cost. It is a way to cut your losses when a trip has turned dangerous.

Team levels and artifacts. Each trip to the same destination builds up that team's stars, up to 5, making later trips safer and luckier. There are 10 artifacts to find, two per destination, and each one adds 1% to every expedition's rewards for good. Rare legendary finds pay about six times the cost.

Can I share GIFs and pictures in chat?

Yes. Paste a link to a GIF or picture from Giphy, Tenor, Imgur or Reddit (i.redd.it) into World Chat or your alliance chat, and it shows as the picture. On Giphy, the normal share link works. On Tenor or Imgur, right-click the GIF and choose Copy image address. Links from other websites stay as plain text. Don't like pictures? Tap the 🖼 button at the top of the chat to show links instead.

Can I design my own flag?

Yes — on the Government page. Pick from 34 layouts (stripes with 3–15 bands, crosses, saltires, chevrons, cantons, sun rays, mountains and more) and 30 emblems (stars, a ring of stars, crescents, crowns, eagles, anchors, gears and more). You can move the emblem to the centre, the corner or the hoist, change its size and colour, and choose up to four flag colours, including any custom colour. There are 20 ready-made designs to start from and a 🎲 button for a random flag. Your flag flies next to your name on the globe, in chat, on your profile and on your public nation page.

Can I change my map colour?

Yes, once a day: open the Map colour tab under Flag & colours on the Government page. It changes the colour of your land on the globe and of your name everywhere.

What is the Black Market?

A shadowy market you open from the Black Market button beside World market on the Economy page. Anyone can sell food, oil, steel, goods or munitions at a price they choose, with no name attached; the goods leave your stockpile while they are listed, and anything unsold comes back after 48 hours (or when you withdraw it). Up to 5 listings at a time.

Only nations shut out of the world market can buy: rogue states, nations under a World Council trade embargo, and nations whose ports are blockaded. It is how they keep a war going.

Both sides take a risk. Listing has a 4–9% chance of being caught, buying a 6–12.5% chance of being intercepted (bigger deals are riskier). Whoever is caught loses the goods, pays a fine of half the deal's value (at least $1M; anything their treasury can't cover goes on their debt), and is named in World Chat. A caught seller also gains 5 aggression. When a buyer is caught the seller isn't paid, but stays anonymous. Nobody ever learns who a seller is unless that seller is caught.

What is a rogue state?

A nation that attacks far too often gets treated like a pariah by the whole world. When your aggression reaches 75, everyone is notified and for at least 12 hours:

  • Full embargo: no market trading, no trade agreements, no aid in or out.
  • All your allies break off the alliance, and nobody can ally with you.
  • Tax revenue −20%, GDP −15%, slower research, and approval and stability keep falling.
  • Every other nation attacks you at +15% strength and gains no aggression for doing it.

The status lifts only once the 12 hours have passed and your aggression has dropped below 50 — otherwise it's extended by 3 hours at a time. Rogue states show a ☠ Rogue badge everywhere. The way out: stop attacking and let it decay.

The endgame — can my nation die?

Can a nation be destroyed?

Yes. A nation that loses a war badly enough can fall:

  • Last stand. When a nation is down to only its capital (whether it lost its other provinces or never had any) and is at war, it is warned and gets 24 hours to make peace, rally allies, fortify or retake land.
  • Siege. After that, enemies at war with it can besiege the capital, which defends at 1.5× strength. AI nations get no warning period: their capital can be besieged as soon as they are down to it and war has been declared.
  • The fall. If the capital is taken, the nation is conquered. The conqueror gains the capital, a quarter of its treasury and half its stockpiles, and the fall is announced to the world and recorded under Fallen nations on the Rankings page.
What happens to me if my nation falls?

You keep your account. You'll see a Your nation has fallen screen showing who conquered you and how long you lasted, and you can found a new nation immediately — with fresh new-nation protection.

What if my nation only has its capital?

If someone declares war on a nation that holds only its capital, that capital can be besieged 24 hours after the declaration. You're warned immediately, so you have time to make peace, call on allies, fortify, or expand. New nations are still protected for their first 48 hours, and much stronger nations still can't attack you.

Can a big nation attack me?

Yes, once your 48-hour new-nation protection ends. Protect yourself with alliances (allies send troops to defend you), fortifications, a strong military and the World Council (ask it for a ceasefire or a security guarantee) — and remember that a nation down to its capital always gets a 24-hour warning before it can be besieged.

Diplomacy, the Council & world events

How do alliances and trade agreements work?

Both sides must agree. Allies send 10% of their troops to help defend you, and allies trade automatically — both get the full trade-agreement bonus without a separate deal (trade bonuses count up to 5 partners). There's no limit on how many trade agreements you can sign, so you never have to cancel one to welcome a new partner; your 5 best partners (7 with a Grand Global Port) give the bonus. Trade agreements give both nations more revenue, GDP, approval and better prices. Peace also needs both sides to agree; war needs only one.

What if I'm at war with someone who stopped playing?

Offer them peace. If they haven't played for over a day and don't answer within 2 hours, peace is signed for them automatically and the war ends. Players who are still active always decide for themselves.

Will an AI nation accept my peace offer?

AI nations answer within a few minutes and judge the offer on how the war is going. They accept when they are losing — land lost, outmatched, low approval — or when a war has dragged on, and refuse when they think they are winning. Warlords are the most stubborn; merchants and isolationists make peace most easily. If they refuse, you'll get a notification saying why, and they reconsider every couple of hours as the war changes. AI nations that are losing may also offer peace themselves.

How do I message other players?

Use Mail (the envelope at the top, or More → Mail on phones), or the Message button on any leader's profile. Mail is private. Use World Chat for public conversation. The red number on the envelope shows unread letters, and Delete all clears your inbox or sent box in one go.

How do I mention someone in chat?

Type @ followed by their nation's name (for example @Zeiro Kingdom) or tap @ Mention on their profile. They get a notification, and messages that mention you are highlighted. Tap any name in chat to open that nation's profile.

What is the World Council?

Open it from the gold World Council banner at the top of the Diplomacy page. It is the world's parliament. Any nation of Mayor rank or above can propose a resolution for $1,000,000 ($10,000,000 to propose anonymously); every player's nation gets one vote (AI nations don't vote); it passes with more yes votes than no and at least 3 votes in favor. Penalties need more: sanctions need two-thirds of the vote and at least 5 yes, and the other penalties (embargo, arms embargo, asset freeze, condemnation, inspections) at least 4 yes. Whoever proposes a penalty also pays a deposit of 1% of their treasury, refunded if it passes. Accounts playing from the same network count as one vote, and penalties (sanctions, embargo, asset freeze, condemnation) can only target a nation that has attacked another player, used nuclear weapons or been caught spying in the last 48 hours, or is very aggressive. Striking back at someone who attacked you first doesn't count, fighting AI nations doesn't count, and AI nations themselves can't be voted penalties. A nation can face at most one penalty vote proposed by players every 24 hours. Voting lasts up to 3 hours, but closes early as soon as the result can no longer change (counting players active in the last 3 days who haven't voted yet), so nobody waits around. There are 21 resolutions in four groups.

You can tick Propose anonymously (it costs $10,000,000 instead of $1,000,000): the resolution is announced as coming from "an anonymous nation", and no player can see who proposed it. The Council doesn't raise votes on its own; every resolution comes from a player.

Measures against a nation

  • Impose sanctions: the target becomes a rogue state for at least 12 hours.
  • Trade embargo (24 h): no world market and no trade agreements.
  • Arms embargo (36 h): no new warships or aircraft, and recruiting costs double.
  • Freeze foreign assets (24 h): a quarter of its treasury is frozen, then returned.
  • Formal condemnation: −10 approval, −8 stability, +20 aggression, and AI nations shun it for 24 hours.
  • Nuclear inspections (72 h): a warhead still being assembled is dismantled and no new one can be started. Only for nations with a nuclear program.

Support for a nation

  • Ceasefire order (12 h): no battles, strikes or nuclear launches between the target and its enemies. The wars stay declared.
  • Security guarantee (48 h): its provinces defend 20% better, and attackers gain double aggression.
  • Humanitarian aid: every nation that votes yes sends 1% of its treasury (up to $2M) and 5% of its food (up to 300). The target also gains health and stability.
  • Lift sanctions: ends any embargo, arms embargo, asset freeze, condemnation and rogue status, and lowers aggression to 30. Not within 72 hours of a nuclear strike. A sanctioned nation can petition for this itself once every 12 hours.

Worldwide treaties

  • Climate accord, Arms reduction treaty, Free trade pact, Peacekeeping mandate (24 h each).
  • Non-proliferation treaty (48 h): no new nuclear programs or warheads.
  • Global health initiative (24 h): health rises and pandemics are half as deadly. Every nation chips in 0.5% of its treasury.
  • Science cooperation (24 h): research is 25% cheaper.
  • Food security program (24 h): +20% food output, food 15% cheaper.
  • Debt relief: 20% of every nation's debt is forgiven, and inflation is 1 point higher for 24 hours. At most once every 3 days.
  • Tariff war (24 h) and Free market week (72 h).

Each worldwide bonus (climate, arms reduction, free trade, peacekeeping, health, science, food, tariffs) can pass at most once every 3 days, and the free market week once every 5 days, so none of them can be kept running non-stop.

Pacts and your own resolutions

  • Collective defense pact (72 h): everyone who votes yes becomes a member. If anyone attacks a member, every other member automatically declares war on the attacker. Choose whether it covers attacks by AI nations only or by anyone. A member who attacks a fellow member is expelled, and a member allied with the attacker still goes to war (the alliance ends).
  • Custom resolution: write your own (up to 200 characters) and choose 24, 48 or 72 hours. If it passes it is listed as in force with every nation that supported it. The game cannot enforce typed text, so it is a public commitment. AI nations do not vote on it.

Measures on a nation show as 🏛 badges on its profile, and on your Overview if they apply to you.

My nation is a rogue state or under sanctions. How do I get back to normal?

Stop attacking: aggression fades by 6 points an hour (online or offline), and rogue status ends once at least 12 hours have passed and your aggression is below 50.

Or petition the World Council from the World Council page. It's free, works at any rank, and you can petition once every 6 hours. If a majority votes yes (with at least 3 yes votes), your embargo, frozen assets (returned to you), condemnation, sanctions and rogue status end at once, and your aggression drops to 30.

Or pay off the Council: right below the petition, you can make it all go away instantly, no vote needed. It costs a quarter of your treasury (at least $50M), double if you're a rogue state, and each payoff within 7 days doubles the price of the next one. The money is gone for good, every nation is told you bought your way out, and it's not possible within 72 hours of a nuclear strike.

If you are a rogue state because you launched a nuke, neither a petition nor a payoff can end it. It ends only on its own, after the minimum time and once your aggression is below 50.

Only players vote, so it helps to make peace and stop attacking first. A petition isn't possible within 72 hours of launching a nuclear strike.

Does the World Council act on its own?

No. Every resolution is proposed by a player, either openly for $1,000,000 or anonymously for $10,000,000, and every player nation then votes on it.

Aggressive nations still face automatic consequences outside the Council: every attack raises your aggression, and at 75 you become a rogue state with a full embargo (see What is a rogue state?).

What are world events?

Every 10–18 hours a global event hits every nation at once for about 8 hours — Global Recession, Pandemic, Oil Crisis, Commodity Boom, Technology Revolution, Crop Failure, Financial Crisis or an Era of Peace — and every leader gets a decision on how to respond.

Building, alliances & world events

What are World Wonders?

Six wonders on the Prestige page that only one nation in the whole world can own: the Orbital Space Station, World Bank Headquarters, Great Wall of Nations, Fusion Power Grid, United Nations Plaza and New Silk Road. They cost $200B to $400B, paid in four stages six hours apart (three hours in a season). Several nations can race for the same wonder: the first to finish owns it for good and its bonus lasts forever, and everyone else racing for it gets half their money back. Each owned wonder adds 2,000 to your score. You need the rank of Senator and can build one at a time.

Why did my GDP, approval or another number go up or down?

Tap the number itself at the top of the screen. A window opens showing how much it changed in the last 24 hours, what is pushing it up or down right now (your tax rate, wars, shortages, unemployment and so on, with how much each one counts), what happened in the last day, and what you can do about it, with a button that takes you to the right page.

What is deep storage?

Three more warehouse levels, on the Development page, for nations that have already built warehouses to level 5. Each level adds 36 hours of storage for food, oil, steel and goods (warehouse levels add 24).

They are very expensive and they cost upkeep: level 1 is $1T to build and $300M an hour, level 2 is $2.5T and $750M an hour more, level 3 is $6T and $1.8B an hour more. The upkeep is charged for as long as the level stays open. You can close a level at any time to stop paying for it, but the building cost is not refunded.

What is the World Stock Exchange?

A market on the Stock Exchange page (from the rank of Elder) where you buy and sell shares in five companies: Meridian Oil, Titan Steel, Aegis Defense, Nova Technologies and Harvest Foods, plus the World Powers Index fund, which is the average of the five.

Prices. Prices move all the time, every few seconds, like a real market, and the chart updates live. Each step is a fair coin: as likely up as down, by the same amount. World events nudge prices too (a war lifts defense, a nuclear strike sinks almost everything, a food shortage lifts Harvest Foods), by at most 2% a day.

Orders. You choose how much to spend or how much to sell. The order fills at the next price, a few minutes later, and you can cancel before then at no cost. Every trade pays a 1.5% fee, which is destroyed.

Limits. A nation can hold shares worth up to 30% of its GDP, place 20 orders an hour and have 6 waiting at once. Shares count as money for the World Bank holding tax, and a nation that captures your capital takes 25% of your shares.

Is it a way to get rich? No. Because every step is a fair coin and every trade pays a fee, no way of timing the market wins on average. You can win big or lose big on any trade, like at the casino. Play it for fun.

Can the stock exchange be rigged or cheated?

It was built so that it cannot be:

Nobody knows the next price. It is drawn fresh at the moment of each step and is not stored anywhere beforehand. Not other players, not the admins, not the game's owner.

Your order never fills at the price on screen. It fills at the next one. So reacting to news faster than others, or causing the news yourself (declaring a war to lift defense shares, say), earns nothing: the price has already moved by the time you buy.

Trades never move a price. A huge order and a tiny one get exactly the same price. Nobody can pump a share up, crash it, or pass money to a friend or a second account through it.

Events are capped below the fees. World events can move a company at most 2% up and 2% down in a day, which is less than the 3% it costs to buy and sell. We tested the worst case, a player who controls every event in the world, and they still lose money on average.

The record cannot be rewritten. Every price step carries a fingerprint built from the step before it. Anyone can check the chain from the link at the bottom of the Stock Exchange page.

What are rebel camps?

Rebels set up camp on unclaimed land (up to 6 at a time, with a new one every couple of hours). You see them as tent markers on the globe and in a list on the Military page. Anyone can raid a camp: there is no war, no anger from other nations, and all you can lose is some troops and a small cost in cash, oil and food.

The garrison is sized to your own nation, so a camp is a fair fight whether you are small or large. There are three sizes: outposts, camps and strongholds. Bigger ones are harder and pay more. The raid window shows your chance to win before you commit. Win and you take cash, some resources and experience, and the camp is gone for everyone. Lose and the camp stays. You can raid once every 30 minutes, and unclaimed land with a camp on it cannot be settled until the camp is cleared. It is the safest way to learn how battles work.

What are war games?

A private one-hour battle between two players. On the Military page, invite another player nation. The moment they accept, both screens turn red and the battle begins.

Your real nations freeze. They stay exactly as they are for the whole battle: nothing happens to them and nobody else can attack, strike or spy on them.

The battle is real. You fight on a map with only your two nations, already at war, with the troops, aircraft, ships, money and upgrades you have right now. Attack provinces, launch missiles and air strikes, use heavy weapons, recruit and build as usual. Nuclear weapons, the casino, diplomacy and projects are switched off during the battle, and capitals cannot fall.

When it ends. After one hour, or when either of you presses End battle, both of you get a full summary: who won, every attack and strike, and what each side lost. Then your real nations come back exactly as you left them, and every timer carries on from where it stopped.

What are grand projects?

National gambles at the bottom of the Operations & Projects page. There are 13, in four sizes:

  • Starter ($500M to $8B, from the rank of Elder): Colossal Monument, High-Speed Rail, Vaccine Program, World-Class Universities.
  • Major ($20B to $60B): Human Genome Project, Super Collider, Moon Landing.
  • Mega ($100B to $250B): Fusion Reactor, Mars Colony, Artificial Superintelligence.
  • Legendary ($500B to $5T): Space Elevator, Dyson Swarm, Interstellar Ark.

You pay up front, wait 1 to 48 hours, and then it succeeds or fails. The chance runs from 90% for the smallest down to 25% for the Interstellar Ark. Success gives a bonus that lasts for good, and prestige. Failure burns all of the money, costs 5 approval, and you can try again after 12 hours. Each failure in a row adds 10% to that project's next chance (up to 90%). The chance shown is the real chance. One project at a time. Major projects and above need the rank of Governor. Megaprojects (wonders of the world) are on the same page.

What are prestige units?

On the Military page: Supercarriers (+5% attack, +3% defense each), Nuclear Submarines (+3% attack, +4% defense) and Stealth Bomber Wings (+5% attack). You can have up to three of each. They cost billions plus steel and oil, and they have heavy upkeep every hour ($500M, $300M and $200M each). If your treasury cannot pay, one is scrapped. Decommissioning one returns a quarter of its price.

What do wars and big armies cost?

Every war costs money every hour while it lasts: the nation that declared it pays $10M plus $40 per soldier, $400 per ship and $500 per aircraft, but never more than 0.2% of its GDP an hour, so a small or brand-new nation is never ruined by one war; a defender pays a quarter of that. Make peace to stop paying. Very large armies also cost more per soldier: nothing changes up to 200,000 troops, then the cost per soldier rises gradually to +50% at 2 million. Your current costs are on the Military page.

Can I show off?

Yes. The Show off tab of the Prestige page sells cosmetics that change only how you look: a chat name color (from plain gold to an animated imperial shimmer), a monument in your capital that shows on the globe and your nation card, and a frame around your flag. Buy each once and switch between the ones you own for free.

What is an alliance team?

A team of up to 4 nations with its own name, tag, flag and motto. Open the Alliance page to found one (costs $10M and needs the rank of Mayor) or join one. Your team's tag shows next to your nation's name everywhere.

  • Members trade with each other automatically (same bonus as a trade agreement) and cannot declare war on each other.
  • Joining needs an invitation from the leader, unless the leader marks the alliance as open. Invitations last 72 hours and appear on your Diplomacy and Alliance pages.
  • An alliance team is separate from a two-nation alliance on the Diplomacy page; you can have both.

You can be in only one alliance team at a time; leave yours before joining or founding another. Members get a private Alliance tab next to World Chat (on phones, on the Chat page), where only your team can read and write. It shows the same messages as the board on the Alliance page.

How does the alliance treasury work?

Any member can deposit money (you get a little XP for it, and the Members table shows who paid in what). The leader can send money out to any member; co-leaders can too, up to 25% of the treasury a day (the leader is told about every payment). The treasury pays for joint projects. If the leader disbands the alliance, what's left goes to the leader.

What are joint projects?

Big projects built in stages from the treasury. Each stage costs 2% of the members' combined GDP (at least $25M), and stages are 3 hours apart. The leader or a co-leader picks the project; then any member can build its stages. Some projects build on an earlier one and unlock when it's finished. A finished project helps every member for as long as they stay in the team:

  • Common Market: +6% tax revenue
  • Mutual Defense Network: provinces defend 8% better
  • Joint Research Institute: research 12% cheaper
  • Development Bank: +3% GDP
  • War College: operations earn 15% more XP
  • Alliance Headquarters: +3 approval and +3 stability
  • Shared Energy Grid: 15% less oil burned
  • Industrial Consortium: provinces produce 5% more
  • Medical Corps: health drifts 8 higher
  • Jobs Accord: unemployment drifts 1 point lower
  • Alliance University (after the Research Institute): education drifts 8 higher
  • Trade Federation (after the Common Market): 2% better market prices and 1 more trade partner counted
  • Civil Service Academy (after the Development Bank): government spending 6% cheaper
  • Fortress Line (after the Defense Network): provinces defend another 7% better

  • Sovereign Wealth Fund (after the Trade Federation): +3% GDP and +3% tax revenue

What can a co-leader do?

The leader can name up to 2 co-leaders from the Members table. Co-leaders keep the alliance running when the leader is away: they can send treasury money to members (up to 25% of the treasury a day), start joint projects, and invite or remove regular members. Only the leader can rename the team, change its flag, open it to everyone, hand over leadership, name co-leaders or disband it. If the leader leaves, a co-leader takes over first.

What are team goals?

Every day the team has two goals: run operations together (25 per member) and make decisions together (3 per member). Every member's actions count. Each goal reached pays into the treasury (0.5% of the members' combined GDP, at least $5M) and gives every member 40 XP. Goals reset each day.

What is the limited world market?

The world market can run in two ways, and the game's owner chooses which. In the unlimited market anyone can buy any amount and only the price moves. In the limited market the market holds a real stock of each commodity, and the Economy page shows two extra columns: On the market and You can import.

  • Stock: exports add to it and imports take from it. A price rises as its stock runs low, and a commodity that is sold out cannot be bought until it is restocked.
  • Where stock comes from: nations that export, and foreign suppliers who restock part of the world's shortfall every 10 minutes. The market is filled up again at the start of every season.
  • Daily limit: each nation can import a set amount of each commodity every 24 hours. The limit grows with your provinces and with how much more you use than you make, so the stock is shared out and nobody can empty the market in one order.
  • Emergency food: if your food would run out in under 12 hours, you can import one day of food at three times the normal price. It does not use the market stock or your limit.
  • Trading with players: trade offers and the Black Market are not limited. When the market is short, another nation's surplus is the place to look.

To depend less on imports, raise your own production: develop your provinces and build infrastructure, or keep a smaller army if oil is what you are short of.

How do I compare my nation with others?

Open Rankings. Pick a leaderboard from the row of buttons (score, GDP, army, technology and more), then press Compare beside any nation to add it to the graphs below. You can compare up to six nations at once:

  • The race: each nation over time. Hover or touch for exact values.
  • Who passed whom: world rank over time. Where two lines cross, one nation overtook the other.
  • Strengths and Head to head: you against one rival you choose.
  • The field: every nation by economy and military.
  • Share of the world: your part of the world's economy, land and military.

The graphs over time need history, which is recorded every hour.

How are alliances ranked?

The Alliance leaderboard (on the Alliance and Rankings pages) adds up the scores of every member plus 500 for each finished project.

What is province development?

Instead of only grabbing more land, you can build up the land you have. Each province goes Undeveloped → Farms → Industry → City → Metropolis. Develop from the province card on the World page, or from Economy → Province development.

  • Each level gives that province +30% output, room for 40,000 more people, +40 score and a little more GDP. A metropolis is worth far more than empty land, so a small, well-built nation can outscore a big empty one.
  • Costs grow with each level and with your GDP (higher levels also need steel and goods). One upgrade every 15 minutes. Cities need the rank of Mayor, metropolises Governor.
  • A province taken by an infantry invasion keeps its development; an armored assault wrecks two levels. Disasters knock a level off too.
What are the World Games?

Every few days the World Games run for about a day, with three contests: Athletics (health helps), the Science Expo (tech helps) and the Culture Festival (education helps). Invest any amount from your Overview page; your money is multiplied by those strengths. Gold, silver and bronze in each contest give +5 / +3 / +1 prestige, approval for a day and XP, and the nation with the most prestige from the Games gets +3% GDP for 48 hours. Prestige adds 50 score per point and shows on your nation card.

What are natural disasters?

Every few hours an earthquake, hurricane, wildfire or flood hits a real province somewhere in the world (the kind fits the land). The nation loses 4% of its people and 5 stability, the province loses a development level, and its output is mostly lost for 12 hours. Every other nation sees the appeal on its Overview page and can send relief: the money goes straight to the victim, and donors earn up to 3 prestige per disaster, XP and a little approval. Every 20% of an appeal you fund also lowers your aggression by 1, so funding a whole appeal yourself takes −5 off. If the appeal is fully funded, half the lost people come back.

Can I upgrade the Golden Dome?

The dome has four missile batteries at the corners of your capital province (zoom in to see them). The shield itself is not drawn on the globe. When it stops a missile, the two nearest batteries fire interceptors that meet it in the air.

The dome only protects provinces near your capital: 600 km at level 1, then 750, 900, 1,050 and 1,200 km at level 5. Missiles aimed at provinces farther away are not intercepted. The Military page shows how many of your provinces are covered.

Yes. Once your dome is built, upgrade it from Military → Golden Dome, up to level 5. Each level stops more missiles and has a better chance against a nuclear missile: level 1 stops 75% and 40%, level 2 80% and 50%, level 3 85% and 58%, level 4 89% and 65%, level 5 92% and 72%. Upgrades cost $300M–$1.2B plus steel, goods and oil, take 6–12 hours (your dome keeps working at its old level meanwhile), and levels 2–5 need tech 320, 340, 370 and 400. Upkeep rises by half for every level (level 5 costs about $24M an hour).

Can I give up my nuclear weapons?

Yes. On Military → Nuclear, tap Decommission. Your deterrent (and any warhead) is dismantled for good, upkeep and inspection fees stop, and 20% of the program's cost comes back from the materials. Your people and the world celebrate: approval +12 (+18 with a warhead), stability +6, aggression −20, +6 approval and +4 stability for 48 hours, the Peacemaker achievement, and every other nation gains 2 approval. Your capital loses its nuclear protection, and you can't start a new program for 7 days.

Can more than one nation nuke the same country?

Yes. Any nation at war with a country can strike it with an armed warhead, even if someone else just did. Every strike adds its own damage. The country that was hit can strike back at any of its attackers within 72 hours as a second strike, which doesn't make it a rogue state.

Can I watch a nuclear strike again?

Yes. Every nuclear launch from the last 12 hours is listed on the World page under "Nuclear strikes". Press ▶ Watch replay to see the missile fly on the globe again. Replays are only a show: nothing is damaged a second time.

Can a nuclear program go wrong?

Yes. Any nation with a nuclear program can suffer a nuclear accident, a Chernobyl. It's rare (about once a month, never twice in a week, a little more likely with an armed warhead), completely random, and nothing can prevent it. A reactor failure at one of your provinces releases a radioactive cloud everyone can see on the globe for 48 hours. You lose 5% of your population, food output falls 30% while crops are contaminated, stockpiles are spent on the clean-up (40% of food, 20% of steel and goods, 10% of oil) and the clean-up costs 2% of GDP. Stability −10, approval −8, health −10.

Can I move my capital?

Yes, once every 72 hours. Select one of your provinces on the World page and tap Move capital here. It's a huge undertaking: 12.5% of your GDP (at least $60M), plus 1,500 steel, 1,000 goods and 750 oil. Stability falls 25, approval 12 and morale 15, and for 24 hours your nation is weakened while the government settles in: −20% tax revenue, −15% defense in every province and −5% GDP. Your Golden Dome is left behind and dismantled, so you'll have to build (and upgrade) a new one, and the new capital only gets its full defense bonus after 12 hours. You must have held the province for at least 24 hours, and it can't be a disaster zone or full of separatists. Your old capital becomes a normal province. You can also give your nation a new name when you move the capital.

Can my provinces break away?

Yes, if you neglect your nation. With stability below 35 and five or more provinces, your outlying provinces may declare independence; below 25 it happens quickly. A province that breaks away becomes a small AI nation (marked 🏴 Breakaway) that anyone can conquer, or it becomes free land. Enemy spies can also arm separatists in one of your provinces for 24 hours. Keep stability up and about 800 troops per province to stay whole; you'll get a warning when unrest starts. Being at war and having high aggression makes every kind of breakaway more likely: +25% per war (up to three) and up to +100% at 100 aggression, so far-flung provinces of a warmonger are the first to go.

How do trade routes work?

Every trade agreement, ally and alliance teammate is a trade route. A route counts once when it's new and grows to count twice after 7 days, so loyal partners pay more (tax, GDP and market prices). You can hold as many routes as you like, and you benefit from your 5 best (7 with a Grand Global Port). Breaking a route older than a day, by cancelling it or declaring war, costs you up to 5% of your treasury (paid to the partner as compensation), 3 approval and 3 stability.

Are there treaties like a nuclear weapons ban or a world truce?

Yes, players can propose them: a Nuclear weapons ban (no launches, dirty bombs or tests for 24 hours), a Tariff war (market fees double and trade routes pay half for 24 hours), a Free market week (cheap trading, +3% tax and +2% GDP for 72 hours) or a World truce (no new wars may be declared for 24 hours), along with the older treaties. Some can only come up now and then, so nobody can re-pass them every time they run out: a World truce once a week, a Nuclear weapons ban once every 3 days and a Non-proliferation treaty once every 4 days.

What is a world crisis?

Every few days the whole world faces a threat: a pandemic, an asteroid, first contact with an alien probe, or a Rising Power on the march. It shows at the top of your Overview, and every nation makes one choice:

  • Cooperate: pay into the world effort, as much as you like, as often as you like.
  • Exploit: take an instant profit (usually 3% of your GDP) and +10 aggression. This can't be undone, and every nation can see who exploited.

If the world raises the goal before the deadline (or brings down the Rising Power), the threat is beaten: every contributor gets +3% GDP and +3 approval for 24 hours and 50 XP. The top 3 contributors get 50%, 35% and 25% of what they paid back, up to 300 XP, +8% tax revenue and +4 stability for 48 hours, and the World Hero achievement. Nations that exploited lose approval and stability. AI nations take part too, but they never pay more than 45% of the goal, so players decide the outcome.

If the world fails: a pandemic costs every nation 4% of its population and 8% of GDP for a day; an asteroid devastates one province and an impact winter cuts GDP 10% for a day (bunker sellers are spared); the alien probe leaves; the Rising Power grows 30% stronger. In the Rising Power crisis, every battle you win against it counts toward your contribution.

Intelligence

What can a rich nation spend its money on?

Four things built for the late game, none of which raises your income: the weekly World Auction for a unique title and badge (Prestige → World races), the five-stage Space Race where the first nation to finish each stage enters the Hall of Fame (Prestige → World races), your alliance headquarters, which strengthens joint attacks and defense for every member (Alliance page), and mercenaries for a day of extra attack or defense (Military page). Money left sitting in a very large treasury may be taxed by the World Bank, so spending it is better than hoarding it.

How do I take a province from a much stronger nation?

Not alone. Use two things together. Battering: every strong attack that lands (missiles, bombardment, artillery, air and drone strikes, nukes) cuts the defense of the province it hits by 15% for 6 hours, up to 60%, and follow-up strikes keep landing on that same province. Joint attacks: on the Military page, allies can order 25%, 50% or 100% of their army to join the assaults of allies on nations they are also at war with. Their strength is added to the attack and they share the losses. The war planner shows the battered provinces (💥) and which allies will join before you launch.

What are the earthquakes, eruptions and tsunamis in a Chaos world?

Every nuclear detonation in a Chaos world fills the Planet stress meter, and nukes landing close together fill it faster. The fuller it is, the likelier a catastrophe strikes somewhere in the world: an earthquake (up to 6 provinces lose a development level and their owners lose 3% of their troops), a volcanic eruption (one province loses everything built there and its neighbours are scorched) or a tsunami (up to 8 coastal provinces lose a development level and their owners lose 15% of their warships). Each catastrophe lets some of the pressure out. None of it carries over to the real world.

What is sudden death in a Chaos world?

The last few minutes of a Chaos world. Every shield drops, timers run twice as fast again, and whoever is in the lead carries a bounty: each province taken from them counts double in the standings. The top 3 at the end keep a badge on their real nation, plus 5, 3 and 2 prestige when at least 3 players gained land.

What changed with the bigger map?

When Season 1 ended, the world map grew from 961 to 1,711 provinces. The land is the same: big provinces were split into two or more smaller ones, and Europe got much more detail. Every old province kept its name and place.

You kept all your land. If a province you held was split, you hold every piece. The extra pieces you received cost no administration, so they don't count against your province limit, and they don't add score. They do produce resources and can be developed like any province. If you lose one and take it back later, it counts like normal land.

The point is room: land nobody held is now many more provinces, so many more nations can settle and grow.

What is the Built-up nation bonus?

It rewards developing your provinces instead of only taking more. Add up the development levels of your provinces (farms 1, industry 2, city 3, metropolis 4) and divide by the number of provinces: that average, from 0 to 4, gives your whole nation up to +32% GDP and +24% production, and makes development count up to double for score.

The bonus reaches full strength at 12 provinces, so a tiny nation can't max it out. A nation of 25 well-developed provinces can now rival one with 60 undeveloped ones. You can see your bonus under Economy → Province development.

What is Chaos world?

Chaos world is a special event the admins can switch on for a short time. The game makes a temporary copy of the world where every weapon is unlocked, every nation gets a huge war chest and a starting army of at least 500,000 troops, 2,000 aircraft and 300 warships (bigger armies are kept as they are, so new nations aren't left with nothing), timers run up to 30× faster and airdrops land every 10 minutes. A red banner shows how long is left.

Nothing in Chaos world is permanent. The real world is saved first, and when the time runs out it comes back exactly as it was, with your cooldowns and shields still having the same time left. Your mail, chat and donations are never affected. Supporter perks that pay into your nation pause until it ends so you don't lose them. A nation founded during Chaos world only lasts until it ends.

Every nation can get 1 or 2 nuclear warheads: with 2, the second is armed straight after the first launch.

World Council peace laws (a world truce, a nuclear ban or the non-proliferation treaty) do not apply inside Chaos world. They are still in force in the real world when it comes back.

How do spies work?

Train agents in Operations, then launch covert operations from the Intelligence page:

  • Steal technology (3 agents, $150k): copy research from a more advanced nation.
  • Sabotage industry (2 agents, $100k): destroy stockpiles, damage infrastructure.
  • Incite unrest (2 agents, $100k): their stability −10 and approval −5.
  • Interfere in election (3 agents, $200k): swing a democracy's next election — or your own.
  • Stir up rebellion (4 agents, $400k): arm separatists in one of their outer provinces; for 24 hours it may break away, far more likely if their stability is low.
  • Plant fake news (2 agents, $150k): their approval −8, stability −4, and +8 aggression as the world blames them.
  • Siphon funds (3 agents, $250k): move 2% of their treasury (up to $50M) into yours.

If your agents are identified, the victim gets a just cause against you for 24 hours: attacking you adds no aggression to them.

Success depends on your agents versus their counter-intelligence, technology and fortifications. Fail, and your agents may be caught publicly: your aggression rises and relations sour.

Voting on missions: tap 👍 Yes or 👎 No on any mission to say whether you like it. It doesn't change the game; it helps decide which missions to improve.

What is the Development page?

Lasting improvements for your nation, on the Development page in the menu. Every bonus is small, and each price is a minimum or a multiple of your own tax income, whichever is higher, so it stays a real choice for every nation.

  • Ministries (levels 1 to 10, each level costs 1.5× the last): Treasury +1% tax revenue per level; Defense +1% attack and defence strength; Interior far provinces 3% less likely to break away; Foreign Affairs 3% less aggression from your attacks, and trade routes pay 5% more; Science research 1.5% cheaper.
  • Infrastructure programs (5 levels each, one level at a time, they last for good). Every level costs a fifth of the full price, steel, goods and time, and gives a fifth of the full effect: power grid, 48 minutes a level, production +1.2% a level (+6% at level 5); rail network, 1.2 hours, army upkeep −1.6% a level (−8%); universities, 1.6 hours, research −1.6% a level (−8%); deep-water ports, 1.2 hours, needs a coast, trade routes +5% and daily import limit +4% a level (+25% and +20%). A program you built before this change counts as level 5.
  • Warehouses and strategic reserve (5 levels): per level, munitions depots hold 30% more, 8% of every stockpile is safe from bombing, sabotage and raids, and your daily import limit is 5% higher.
  • Refineries and processing (5 levels): switch on oil into goods (each oil becomes 0.7 goods) or surplus food into population growth. Each level processes up to 2% of a stockpile an hour, and never the reserve your nation needs to run.
  • Civil defence (5 levels): 6% less damage per level from air strikes, missiles, heavy weapons, dirty bombs and nuclear strikes.
  • Counter-intelligence bureau (5 levels): per level, enemy spy operations are 3 points less likely to succeed against you, and failed ones are 20% more likely to be identified.
How do trade routes work?

On the Development page, open a route with an ally: a nation you have an alliance pact with, or a member of your alliance. After that both of you are paid every hour, each an amount equal to one tax collection of whichever of you earns less. The one-time setup fee is about 5 days of what that route pays you, so every route pays for itself in about 5 days whoever the partner is; the Development page shows each ally's exact price and pay. You can open up to 3 routes of your own, and routes that allies open to you pay you as well (up to 4 routes pay in total). The Foreign Affairs ministry and deep-water ports raise the payment.

A route pays nothing in any hour when either of you is under a naval blockade, and it pauses if you stop being allies or go to war. You only earn while you are playing, like the rest of your economy.

What are reserves?

Part-time soldiers, trained on the Development page. They cost about two-thirds of a regular recruit and under 20% of a soldier's upkeep, and they do not fight or count toward your army until you call them up. Your reserve can hold up to half your army limit.

Call up moves every reservist into your army at once, even above your normal army limit. You can only do it when you are at war or were attacked in the last day, at most once every 30 minutes, and it costs 1 approval. Once called up they are ordinary soldiers with full upkeep.

Can my Golden Dome break?

Yes, if it is knocked out by an EMP and left that way. A knocked-out dome still comes back by itself (after 12 hours at level 1, down to 4 hours at level 5), but once it has been down for 3 hours, every further hour brings an 8% chance that it breaks down completely. A broken dome is gone: you have to build a new one at full price.

To avoid that, press Emergency repair on the dome's panel on your Military page. It brings the dome back at once and costs from $160M (more for higher levels and for nations with a large income). You get a notification when your dome is knocked out and another if it breaks.

What games are in the World Casino?

Slots (nine machines, with a shared jackpot and a weekly tournament), blackjack, roulette, baccarat, craps, high-low dice, video poker, crash and poker against other nations. Stakes come straight from your treasury.

  • Slots: two old-school three-reel machines and six digital ones, each a different kind of game: Neon Empire (ten paylines and free spins), War Chest (243 ways and a prize crate), Oil Baron (cascading reels with a rising multiplier), Spy Vault (expanding wilds with respins, and safe-cracking), World Tour (six reels of changing height with up to 46,656 ways, and a bonus wheel) and Nuclear Winter (a 6 by 5 pay-anywhere grid with multiplier bombs in free spins). Every machine pays back about 94% over time, and one spin can pay at most 1,000× your stake.
  • Roulette: place chips on as many numbers and outside bets as you like, then spin once. The table limit applies to the total.
  • Baccarat: Player pays 1 to 1, Banker 0.95 to 1 and Tie 8 to 1.
  • Video poker: Jacks or Better with one draw. A royal flush pays 250×.
  • Craps: pass line, don't pass, odds of up to 2× your bet at the true chance, and the field.
  • Crash: a multiplier climbs until it crashes; cash out before it does. About 6 rounds in 100 crash at once, and rounds stop at 100×.
Does it matter which safe or crate I open in a slot bonus?

No. The prizes of a bonus round are drawn by the server the moment the bonus starts, the same way real casino machines work. Opening a different safe or crate shows the same prizes in the same order, and the bonus is paid in full even if you close the page before finishing it.

What are the casino limits?

The minimum bet is $10K. The table limit on every game except poker is $5B, or what you have in your treasury if that is less. Chips go from $10K up to $5B, and you can type any stake in between.

Which slot machines are there?

Nine, chosen with the buttons above the machine. Each pays back about 94% over time on its own reels; the shared jackpot and the weekly tournament add about 1.5% on top. The newest is Victory Wheel: five reels, ten lines, and three or more wheel symbols send you to a 16-wedge wheel worth 8× to 500× your stake (×2 with four wheel symbols, ×4 with five). The first four are:

  • Lucky Sevens (old school): the original three-reel machine. 777 pays 300×.
  • Liberty Bell (old school): three reels with a wild bell that stands in for any symbol and doubles the win (two wilds: ×4). Three wilds pay 250×.
  • Neon Empire (digital): five reels, ten lines. Three or more bonus stars anywhere give 7, 10 or 15 free spins, and every free-spin win pays double.
  • War Chest (digital): five reels, ten lines. Three or more chests anywhere open the prize chest for 5× to 100× your stake (more with 4 or 5 chests).

On the digital machines the pay table shows what 3, 4 or 5 in a row from the left pay, as a multiple of your whole stake. Free spins play by themselves and cost nothing. Auto spin and full screen work on every machine.

What happens if I attack a nation that is in an alliance?

An attack on one is an attack on all. The moment you hit a member of an alliance (a battle, an air or missile strike, a dirty bomb, a nuclear strike, or a heavy weapon traced to you), every other member of that alliance, and any nation with an alliance pact with the victim, gets a just cause against you for 24 hours. They can strike you without gaining aggression, exactly as if you had attacked them. Every one of them is notified, you are told how many nations you just provoked, and it is announced in the world news. Each new attack renews the 24 hours. Members of your own alliance are not affected.

What do dirty bombs and nuclear weapons cost?

They are priced to be a serious decision for every nation. Each has a minimum price and grows with your own tax income, whichever is higher:

  • Dirty bomb: at least $500M, or 4 tax collections.
  • Nuclear deterrent: at least $5B, or 10 tax collections (and still ×1.5 for every nation that already has one).
  • Launchable warhead: at least $25B, or 20 tax collections.

The steel, oil, goods and munitions needed are unchanged, and so is the yearly upkeep. Your Military page and the war room always show your exact price.

How do the duel mini-games work?

Eight quick games of skill on the Intelligence page: Crack the vault, Break the code, War room memory, Sharpshooter range, Missile defense, Pin the capital, Border checkpoint and Market sprint. In the box above the games choose who to play: a player who was online in the last 2 minutes (a live 1v1) or Ghost duel, which is always available.

  • Live duel: they get 30 seconds to accept. You then both play the same puzzle at the same time, and the better score wins at least half the prize, more for a high score. A tie, or a winning score under 30, wins nothing. If they decline or don't answer, it is called off and costs you nothing.
  • Ghost duel: you race another player's recorded run of the same puzzle, straight away. Beat their score to win. The prize is half the size of a live duel prize. If no recorded run is waiting for you, the Academy instructor plays. You never get the same recorded puzzle twice, and never your own.
  • Difficulty: every game has Easy, Medium and Hard. Easy pays 60% of the prize, Hard pays 150%.
  • Costs: the challenger pays a small entry fee (8% of the prize) when the game starts. The player who is challenged pays nothing, and the loser never loses money, generals or anything else.
  • How often: each game pays a prize once every 2 hours. In between you can play friendly rounds (no fee, no prize) against players or ghosts, and practice is free and unlimited.
  • Rematch: both players get a Rematch button on the result screen. A rematch is a friendly round.
  • From anywhere: tap a name in the online list or chat. If they are online, their profile has a Duel button.
  • Opting out: untick the box on your Intelligence page and nobody can challenge you.
What are duel ratings and titles?

Everyone starts with a rating of 1,000 in each of the eight games. Winning a duel raises it and losing lowers it; beating a stronger player moves it more. Ghost duels count for half and only change your own rating. Friendly rounds count too. The same two players can change each other's rating at most three times a day.

The best-rated player of each game (with at least three rated duels and a rating of 1,020 or more) holds its title until somebody passes them: Master safecracker, Master codebreaker, Master of memory, Sharpshooter, Shield of the nation, Master navigator, Chief inspector and Market wizard. Titles show on the holder's profile. See them all on Rankings → Duels.

Account, chat & fair play

Can I have more than one account?

No — one account per person and per email address. Accounts that look like duplicates are flagged for the administrators and can be banned.

I forgot my password.

Send a message to the game administrator, who can set a new password for you. Once you're back in, change it on the Account page.

How do I change my password, sign out of other devices, or delete my account?

Open the Account page — the person icon at the top of the screen, or More → Account on phones. You can change your password (this signs out your other devices), sign out of every device, or permanently delete your account, nation, chat messages and mail.

Can people cheat?

Every action is checked and calculated by the game server, so results can't be changed from a browser. Suspicious activity is flagged automatically for the administrators.

Someone is being rude. What can I do?

Open their profile (tap their name) and choose Mute — you'll no longer see their chat or receive their mail — or Report, which goes privately to the administrators. Offensive language is filtered automatically. See the Terms for the rules.

Does my phone vibrate when big weapons hit?

On Android phones, yes: when a big weapon lands on the globe (ballistic or hypersonic missile, orbital strike, EMP, stealth bomber) the screen shakes and the phone vibrates, more strongly when it hits your own nation or comes from you, and a nuclear blast gives a long rumble. Turn it off with Vibrate on big hits next to Sound effects in your notification settings. iPhones don't let websites vibrate, so on an iPhone you get the screen shake only.

Who is “🧪 Test” in the online list?

An admin test account. It plays nonstop in a separate copy of the world to check that every feature works and that nothing gives anyone an unfair edge. It has no nation in the real world, can’t attack or trade with anyone, and can’t be messaged.

How do I contact the game staff?

Click ❓ Helpdesk at the start of the Leaders online list (or at the top of the chat sidebar), or pick Helpdesk in the “To” box when you write mail. Ask anything about the game or report a problem with your account. The staff answer in your Mail inbox, you get a notification when they do, and you can reply to keep the conversation going.

Your question is private: only the game owner sees it. Even if you are muted or your account is under investigation, you can still write to the Helpdesk.

Where can I suggest ideas, and how do I know what happened to them?

Post them in the Dev corner forum (Ideas, Balance, Bugs or General), where other players can reply and 👍 them. When the team decides on a suggestion it's marked ✅ Accepted, 🛠 Done or ❌ Rejected, usually with a short reason. You get a notification for your own, and the 📜 Decisions tab lists every decided suggestion, newest first.

How do invites work?

Your personal invite link is on the Overview page and under Account. When a friend joins through it and founds a nation, they start with an extra $25M and you get 10 tax collections of your own income (at least $10M). When your friend reaches the rank of Governor, you get another 40 tax collections (at least $50M). You can be rewarded for up to 20 friends. No reward is given when the new player is on the same network as you.

Can I share my nation?

I found a bug. How do I report it?

Tap Report a bug on the Account page, the Guide or the More menu on phones. Describe what happened and attach a screenshot if you can — your device type and any errors are included automatically and go straight to the administrator.

Are there special events?

Yes. Besides random world events, the administrators run special events such as Double XP, double income or faster operations weekends. A green banner shows while one is running, and everyone is notified when it starts.

Is it free?

Yes, completely free.

How can I support the game?

Thank you! Go to worldpowersgame.com/donate or tap Donate in the game. Sign in first so your donation is linked to your nation and your supporter perks switch on by themselves. The Fair play page shows where the money goes.

What do supporters get? Is it pay-to-win?

Your supporter level is set by everything you have donated in total: ⭐ Supporter from $5, 🌟 Patron from $20, 👑 Benefactor from $50.

  • Every supporter: a badge beside their name and Rush orders that finish an operation's timer at once (1 a day, 5 for Patrons, 10 for Benefactors).
  • Patrons and Benefactors: a gold monument on their capital on the globe, Rapid redeployment, which clears attack, strike and spy timers and the army regroup (2 a day, 4 for Benefactors), and a weekly war chest (5% of GDP between $10M and $100M; 10% between $25M and $250M for Benefactors).

Separately, anyone can reserve their nation name forever with a one-time payment on the Donate page: nobody else can ever use that name, in any season.

The perks are capped so effort still decides who wins. Nobody can buy troops, land or protection, and a supporter can be beaten like anyone else. Every donation is checked with PayPal, and a refunded or charged-back donation stops counting.
