Getting started

What is World Powers?

A free, real-time strategy game played in your browser with everyone else at the same time. You lead one nation on a real map of the world: run its economy, trade, form alliances, vote in the World Council, hold elections — and decide when it's worth going to war.

How do I start?
  • Create an account (one per email address) and you'll be taken to Found your nation.
  • Tap an unclaimed province on the globe — it must be at least two provinces away from any other nation.
  • Pick a name, a color and a form of government, then tap Establish nation.
  • Go to Operations and tap Collect Taxes. The Getting started checklist on your Overview walks you through the rest.
Can I try it without making an account?

Yes — tap Play as a guest on the home page or the sign-in screen. You get a full nation straight away. To keep it on any device and use mail, open the Account page and tap Save your account (add an email and password). Guest games that are never saved are deleted after 7 days without play.

Where should I start my nation?

Location matters, because resources follow the real world:

ProvinceProduces every 10 minutes
Farmland30 food
Oil fields (Middle East, Russia, Texas…)15 oil, 5 food
Mining region15 steel, 5 food
Industrial region12 goods, 5 food
Plains15 food + cash
Desert4 food + cash

Rivers add 5 food, mountains add 4 steel and coasts add 3 goods. Mountains (+25%) and rivers (+15%) are easier to defend, and a coastal start gives you 2 warships and access to the sea.

Am I safe when I'm new?

Yes. New nations are protected from attack for 48 hours (unless they attack someone first). After that, you still can't be attacked by a nation that is much stronger than you — see War.

What happens while I'm offline?

Your nation keeps producing for 2 hours after you were last online, then pauses until you return. Nothing is taken from you for being away, but others can still attack you if you're at war.

What is the login streak?

Claim International Aid (in Operations) every day to build a streak. Each day in a row adds +15% to the grant, up to ×1.75 — and every 7th day pays ×2.5 plus bonus experience. Claim within 48 hours of your last claim to keep the streak going. Your streak is shown on the Overview.

What happens when I come back after a while?

If you've been away for an hour or more, a While you were away summary shows attacks on you, unread mail, decisions waiting, notifications and what happened to your nation.

What are the nations marked “AI”?

Computer-controlled nations, each with a personality (merchant, expansionist, warlord or isolationist). They play by the same rules as people (except the Rising Power, below), vote in the Council, can be traded with, allied, attacked — and conquered. They will also declare war on real players whose armies they can match. They gradually step aside as more real players join.

They fight like players too: they attack neighbors of similar strength (never much weaker or brand-new nations), hit back at anyone who attacks them — even from overseas — with invasions, missiles and air strikes, gang up on rogue states, and refuse to trade with nations the World Council has condemned or sanctioned.

What is the Rising Power?

One AI nation, marked ⚠ Rising Power, is a superpower the world has to stop together. It is honest about being helped: foreign backers keep its money, technology and military growing toward about 90% of the strongest player's.

How to beat it: the damage you do sticks. It can't rebuild for 2 hours after it's attacked or struck, and once 3 or more nations are at war with it, its backers pull out completely: no more money or weapons, and its forces slowly wear down. Its air force can never be more than 1.5× the biggest player air force, and its fortifications at most 2 levels above the best-fortified player. Alone you'll struggle; together you can bring it down.

  • It declares war on the strongest players it can reach, invades and launches air strikes.
  • It builds a nuclear deterrent, then a launchable warhead, and will fire it at a player it has been at war with for at least 6 hours.
  • Other AI nations rarely vote against it in the World Council, so players have to show up and vote: nuclear inspections can dismantle its warhead while it is being assembled, and sanctions or an embargo slow it down.
  • Team up: a Collective Defense Pact brings every member into the war when one is attacked.
  • A nuclear deterrent normally protects a capital from siege, but not a rogue state's. Once the Rising Power launches a nuke or is sanctioned into a rogue state, its capital can be taken and the nation destroyed.

When it falls, the whole world is told, and another power may rise 48 hours later. Admins can turn it off.

Playing on a phone

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. The main pages are in the bar at the bottom of the screen; everything else is under More (Military, Economy, Government, Intelligence, Diplomacy, World Council, Mail, Rankings, Guide, FAQ and Account).

How do I install it like an app?

Tap More → Install as an app. It then opens full-screen from your home screen with no browser bars.

  • Android (Chrome): the button installs it directly, or use the ⋮ menu → Install app.
  • iPhone: open the game in Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen → Add.
The text is too small.

Tap More → Larger text. Your phone remembers the setting.

How do I get notifications on my phone or computer?

Everything that happens to you appears under the bell at the top — tap × to dismiss one, or Clear all. Tap the bell at the top → Turn on phone & desktop notifications, then allow them. You can choose exactly which alerts you get (attacks, war declarations, mail, elections, operations ready, and more). On iPhone, notifications only work after installing the game to your Home Screen.

How do I rank up?

Every operation and decision earns experience (XP). Each rank adds 4 to your province limit, +10% output and better defense.

Chieftain (0) → Elder (100) → Mayor (300) → Governor (700) → Senator (1,500) → Prime Minister (3,000) → President (6,000) → Sovereign (12,000) → Emperor (25,000) → Supreme Leader (50,000) → World Power (100,000).

At Mayor you can declare war and propose World Council resolutions.

How are nations ranked?

The Rankings page orders every nation by its score, which adds up:

  • 100 per province
  • 0.4 per soldier, 8 per warship and 6 per aircraft
  • technology (each level counts a little less than the one before)
  • 0.1 per XP, 1 per 2,000 people, and 1 per $500,000 of GDP

You can also rank by economy, society or military. The page charts the race over the last day, three days or week, and shows each nation's share of world GDP, military power and land, the biggest movers, and how many places every nation rose or fell since yesterday. History is recorded once an hour.

What are decisions?

About every 40 minutes an issue lands on your desk — strikes, scandals, disasters, opportunities — with several options and their effects shown. There are 249 different scenarios, and some only appear in certain situations (at war, in debt, under sanctions…).

How do I get more land?

Use Expand Borders to settle an unclaimed province next to your territory (dashed outlines on the map). With warships you can settle across the sea. Your rank limits how many provinces you can hold. You can also take land in war.

Economy

How does the economy work?

Your GDP grows with population, technology, infrastructure, education, health, trade and stability, and shrinks with unemployment, inflation, war and sanctions. Tax revenue is a share of GDP: part of it arrives automatically every 10 minutes (roughly enough to cover normal government spending), and the Collect Taxes operation brings in the rest — so the more often you collect, the richer you get. One game year passes every real day.

Can I pay off my national debt?

Yes. On the Economy page, type an amount next to bonds and press Repay debt (leave it empty to repay as much as your treasury allows). Borrowing isn't bad: issuing bonds to expand early is a strong strategy as long as you keep collecting taxes. Interest is charged every 10 minutes, and the rate rises as debt grows past 40% of GDP. You can borrow up to 120% of GDP.

Avoid two things: letting the treasury go deeply negative (below −20% of GDP your government goes bankrupt — debt is cut, but approval, stability and GDP crash and no one lends to you for 48 hours), and debt above twice your GDP, which also triggers a default.

What does the budget do?

On the Economy page you choose total spending (5–40% of GDP) and how it's split:

  • Military pays troop, ship and aircraft upkeep and keeps morale up. Underfund it and morale falls and soldiers desert.
  • Health raises population growth and approval.
  • Education raises GDP and speeds research.
  • Infrastructure raises GDP and lowers unemployment.

Spending is paid from your treasury every 10 minutes.

What about taxes?

Set the rate (5–50%) on the Government page. Rates above 20% cost approval each time you collect and over time; higher taxes also cool inflation.

Can one player run away with the game?

The game is built so the top stays catchable:

  • Diminishing returns: technology, infrastructure and fortifications give their full boost early on, but each further level adds a little less — and costs a little more.
  • Catch-up research: technology that other nations already know is up to 50% cheaper to research.
  • Development aid: nations far behind the world leader earn up to +50% tax revenue, shrinking as they grow. You'll see it on your Economy page.
  • Army limits: your army can't exceed 4% of your population, and a big army needs a big military budget — underfunded troops lose morale and desert.
Can I borrow money? Can my nation go bankrupt?

Yes and yes. Issue government bonds on the Economy page, up to 120% of your GDP. Interest is charged every 10 minutes and rises as your debt grows; your credit rating runs from AAA down to D.

If debt passes 200% of GDP, or your treasury falls deep into the red, your nation defaults: most of the debt is written off, but the economy crashes, stability and approval collapse, inflation spikes and lenders refuse you for 48 hours.

How do trade and the market work?

Import and export food, oil, steel and goods on the world market. Prices move with what every nation buys and sells over the last 6 hours, and with world events. Use the quick buttons (50, 250, 1K, 5K, Max buy, Max sell) to set an amount in one tap; the preview shows the exact total and the average price per unit before you trade. Big orders move the price as they fill, so very large orders cost more (or earn less) per unit. Max buy picks the most you can import at a fair price while keeping 15% of your treasury; Max sell picks the most you can export before the price drops much. The market gets deeper as the world grows, and no single nation can push a price beyond about double or half on its own. Trade agreements (up to 5, both sides must agree) give both nations +5% revenue, higher GDP and better prices.

What happens if I run out of resources?

No food shrinks your population. No oil weakens your military by 40%. No goods erodes approval. Keep an eye on the Net column on your Overview.

Government, elections & stability

Which form of government should I pick?
  • Democracy: +5% tax revenue, faster research, approval bonus. Elections every 3 days, and wars cost twice the approval.
  • Monarchy: highly stable, small military bonus, no elections.
  • Dictatorship: +15% military strength and war barely dents approval, but lower approval and income.
  • Communist State: recruiting and construction 25% cheaper, lower approval.

You can change at most once every 6 hours; the transition costs 25 stability and 10 approval.

How do elections work?

Democracies vote every 3 days. Your vote share follows approval, unemployment and inflation, plus any campaigning (Election Campaign, up to +12%, opens 24 hours before polling). The Government page shows your projected vote.

  • Lose and a coalition resets your taxes and budget.
  • Lose badly (under 35%) and a new administration also purges ministries.
Can my government be overthrown?

Non-democracies don't hold elections, but if stability and approval both collapse, a revolution can topple the government: it changes form and you lose 30% of your cash and some troops.

War

How do I attack someone?

Tap Attack next to their name — in Diplomacy, on their profile, or on any of their provinces on the map. The war planner opens and walks you through it:

  • Rank of Mayor (300 XP) — needed to wage war.
  • Declare war — one tap in the planner. It's public, and ends any alliance or trade deal with that nation.
  • Mobilize — your forces need 10 minutes after the declaration.
  • Pick a target — the planner lists all their provinces, best targets first, with distance, defense, cost and your chance of winning.
  • Choose how many troops and aircraft to send, and tap Launch attack.
Who can I attack?
  • Not players under new-nation protection (48 hours) — they show a blue “Protected” badge with the time left.
  • There is no limit on attacking smaller nations — so choose your wars and your allies carefully.
  • AI nations have no new-nation protection and can be attacked at any time once you are at war.
  • Not a capital — until that nation is down to its capital alone (see Endgame).

You can attack any province of a nation you're at war with — you don't need to border them. Distance matters, though: see “Do I need to border a nation to attack it?”.

How are battles decided?

Your troops (each aircraft counts as 20 soldiers) times technology, training, morale, fuel and government, against the defender's troops, fortifications, rank, technology, training, morale, terrain, allies' support and how long they've held the land (+5% per day, up to +50%). There's a little luck either way.

Every attack costs $25, 0.1 oil and 0.05 food per soldier (more for distant targets), lowers approval and stability, adds aggression, and your forces regroup for 30–60 minutes (defense −25%).

Do I need to border a nation to attack it?

No. You can attack any province of any nation you're at war with. How far away it is changes how hard it is:

TargetStrengthCost
Borders your landFullNormal
Across a narrow sea−20%, warships carry 100 troops eachNormal
Same continent, further awayUp to −45% with distanceRises with distance (about 2× at 2,500 km)
Overseas (another continent or island)−20% landing, plus the distance penalty; warships carry 75 troops each (at least 5 needed)Rises with distance

For anything by sea, your fleet must defeat theirs first. A captured province becomes a foothold: nearby provinces then count as bordering you. The war planner shows all of this — and your chance of winning — before you commit.

Can I attack a nation on the other side of the world?

Yes. Over land, your army marches there (weaker and costlier with distance). Across an ocean, you need a coastal province and at least 5 warships — each carries 75 troops — and your fleet must beat theirs at sea before you land. Long-distance attacks are hard: build up first, or take a nearby province to use as a foothold.

What happens if I'm attacked and lose?
  • The province becomes theirs, with everything it produced.
  • You lose soldiers (up to about a quarter of your army in a single battle) and possibly aircraft.
  • They seize up to 5% of your treasury.
  • Approval −4, stability −3, and the world hears about it.

Your other provinces, technology, buildings and fortifications are untouched. If you win the defense, you keep the land and the attacker loses far more.

What are generals?

Every victory has a 25% chance to reveal a talented officer — up to three serve at once. There are three kinds:

  • Field marshal: +4% attack strength per star.
  • Defense commander: +4% defense in every province per star.
  • Quartermaster general: each star cuts the distance penalty and cost of long-range attacks by 10%.

Field marshals gain a star for every 3 victories (up to 5). A field marshal can be killed when an attack fails, and a defense commander when a province falls. See yours on the Military page.

What are air and missile strikes?

From the war planner, you can damage an enemy without invading: hit their military, stockpiles or infrastructure.

  • Air strikes use at least 5 aircraft and 3 oil each. Their air force can shoot some down.
  • Missiles need tech level 8 and cost $400,000, 40 steel and 40 oil each. You can fire up to 5 per strike at first. That rises by 1 for every 2 tech levels above 8, and by 1 more each at Senator, President and Emperor rank, up to 10. They can't be intercepted, though fortifications reduce hits.

A strike adds 8 aggression when you start the fight, 3 when you started the war but they are hitting back, and none at all when you are defending yourself or an ally. Strike forces need 30 minutes to rearm.

Every strike is shown on the globe for all players: aircraft fly in formation from the attacker's capital (missiles as fast streaks), with flashes on impact and smoke over the province for about 10 minutes. Everyone gets a short note with a Show me button, and if you are the target your map swings to the strike.

What is a nuclear deterrent?

A very expensive project (tech level 12, rank of Senator, $25,000,000, 1,500 steel, 1,500 oil and 500 goods) found on the Military page. It is never used — but while you have one, your capital cannot be besieged, so your nation can't fall — unless you become a rogue state, which strips that protection. The whole world is alerted, your aggression jumps by 40 (likely sanctions), and it costs $100,000 upkeep every 10 minutes.

Can nukes actually be launched?

Yes — but it's very hard, and the whole world will know. A nation with a deterrent that has been operational for 24 hours, tech level 15, the rank of President, $75,000,000, 3,000 steel, 3,000 oil and 2,000 goods can assemble one launchable warhead. Assembly takes 12 hours and every nation is warned it's coming. Once armed, it can be launched at any nation you are at war with (from their profile).

  • Ground zero: 25% of the population and half the army killed, 40% of navy/air and stockpiles lost, GDP −40%, up to 3 infrastructure levels destroyed — and all tax collection stops for 48 hours.
  • Fallout: every nation with land within 1,600 km loses 6% of its population and health, and has its tax revenue halved for 24 hours.
  • Nuclear winter: the entire world's tax revenue drops 15% for 24 hours.
  • The launcher becomes a rogue state for at least 72 hours, loses all allies, and can't build another warhead for 7 days.
  • Mutually assured destruction: if the target also has an armed warhead, it fires back at the launcher's capital automatically.

Every player sees the detonation flash on screen and a glowing ☢ on the globe for 48 hours. It's all recoverable: taxes resume, and population and the economy rebuild over time. Nukes never capture land or topple a nation by themselves.

Can conquered provinces rebel?

Yes. For 48 hours after you capture a province it is restless. If you don't keep about 800 troops per province you hold, it may rise up and return to its former owner (or break away). The Military page shows your newly conquered land and whether it is garrisoned.

How do I fight back or protect myself?
  • Counterattack: for 24 hours after someone hits you, the strength protections don't shield them from you.
  • Allies automatically send 10% of their troops to your defense.
  • Fortify (stronger defense with every level), train, build aircraft, and hold land longer.
  • Take it to the World Council for sanctions or an embargo.
What are aggression and sanctions?

Every attack and strike adds aggression, which fades slowly over time.

Aggression punishes starting fights, not defending yourself:

  • Starting a war: each ground attack adds 15 and each air or missile strike adds 8.
  • Once they hit back, the nation that declared the war still adds 5 per attack and 3 per strike. It started the war, so it stays the aggressor.
  • Defending: if a nation declared war on you, or hit you or one of your allies in the last 24 hours (any battle, strike or nuke), fighting back adds no aggression at all, so you can never be sanctioned or made a rogue state for defending yourself. The same goes for wars you joined through a Collective Defense Pact, and for a nuclear second strike: firing back at a nation that nuked you in the last 72 hours (or your armed warhead retaliating automatically) doesn't make you a rogue state.
  • Rogue states: attacking a rogue state adds nothing.

Aggression fades by 6 points an hour. At 50 aggression you're automatically under international sanctions: worse market prices, no trade-deal benefits and no new trade agreements. At 75 you are declared a rogue state (see below). The World Council can also vote to sanction you, which makes you a rogue state straight away.

Can the admins or other players cheat?

Players can't: every action is checked and applied by the server, so nobody can edit their own money, troops or timers, and suspicious activity is flagged. For the admins, the Fair play record shows a fingerprint of the exact game code running and the date it last changed, the settings in force, and every admin action that affected the game — nation edits, rollbacks, setting changes, events — logged automatically with exactly what changed.

What are national laws?

On the Government page you can pass laws, each with a real trade-off — for example Conscription (50% bigger recruit batches and a higher troop limit, but lower approval), Universal healthcare, Free education, Press censorship, Green energy, Free market, Martial law and Business tax cuts. Passing a law costs 0.5% of your GDP; repealing is free. You can hold 1 law at first and up to 5 as you rank up, and parliament needs 6 hours between changes.

What are megaprojects (wonders of the world)?

On the Projects page, nations of Mayor rank and above can build wonders: the Global Stock Exchange, Space Program, Great Wall, Olympic Games and Grand Global Port. Each is built in 3–5 stages, 8 hours apart, and gives a permanent bonus. Only one nation in the world can own each wonder — if a rival finishes first, you get half your money back. Capture a nation's capital and you take its wonders.

What are daily missions and achievements?

Every day you get three missions on the Projects page (collect taxes 8 times, win a battle, reach 70% approval…). Each pays cash and 30 XP, and finishing all three pays a bonus. Achievements are permanent badges — First Victory, Conqueror, Empire, Billion-Dollar Economy, Wonder of the World and more — shown on your public nation page.

Can I trade directly with another nation?

Yes. Open a nation's profile and choose Offer a deal: offer cash or resources in exchange for something you want, with no market fees. What you offer is held until they accept, decline, or 24 hours pass (then it comes back to you). AI nations accept deals that are fair at market prices. You can't deal with nations you're at war with, rogue states, or nations under embargo.

What are expeditions?

From the Projects page (Elder rank and up) you can send one expedition at a time — a mountain survey, desert prospecting, jungle expedition, deep-sea expedition or Arctic research station. A few hours later it comes back with resources, treasure, an artifact that lifts national pride, a scientific breakthrough (+1 tech) — or, sometimes, disaster.

Can I design my own flag?

Yes — on the Government page, choose a layout, an emblem and three colors, and write a national motto. Your flag appears next to your nation's name everywhere in the game and on your public nation page.

What is a rogue state?

A nation that attacks far too often gets treated like a pariah by the whole world. When your aggression reaches 75, everyone is notified and for at least 24 hours:

  • Full embargo: no market trading, no trade agreements, no aid in or out.
  • All your allies break off the alliance, and nobody can ally with you.
  • Tax revenue −20%, GDP −15%, slower research, and approval and stability keep falling.
  • Every other nation attacks you at +15% strength and gains no aggression for doing it.

The status lifts only once the 24 hours have passed and your aggression has dropped below 50 — otherwise it's extended. Rogue states show a ☠ Rogue badge everywhere. The way out: stop attacking and let it decay.

The endgame — can my nation die?

Can a nation be destroyed?

Yes. A nation that loses a war badly enough can fall:

  • Last stand. When a nation is down to only its capital (whether it lost its other provinces or never had any) and is at war, it is warned and gets 24 hours to make peace, rally allies, fortify or retake land.
  • Siege. After that, enemies at war with it can besiege the capital, which defends at 1.5× strength. AI nations get no warning period: their capital can be besieged as soon as they are down to it and war has been declared.
  • The fall. If the capital is taken, the nation is conquered. The conqueror gains the capital, a quarter of its treasury and half its stockpiles, and the fall is announced to the world and recorded under Fallen nations on the Rankings page.
What happens to me if my nation falls?

You keep your account. You'll see a Your nation has fallen screen showing who conquered you and how long you lasted, and you can found a new nation immediately — with fresh new-nation protection.

What if my nation only has its capital?

If someone declares war on a nation that holds only its capital, that capital can be besieged 24 hours after the declaration. You're warned immediately, so you have time to make peace, call on allies, fortify, or expand. New nations are still protected for their first 48 hours, and much stronger nations still can't attack you.

Can a big nation attack me?

Yes, once your 48-hour new-nation protection ends. Protect yourself with alliances (allies send troops to defend you), fortifications, a strong military and the World Council (ask it for a ceasefire or a security guarantee) — and remember that a nation down to its capital always gets a 24-hour warning before it can be besieged.

Diplomacy, the Council & world events

How do alliances and trade agreements work?

Both sides must agree. Allies send 10% of their troops to help defend you, and allies trade automatically — both get the full trade-agreement bonus without a separate deal (trade bonuses count up to 5 partners). Trade agreements give both nations more revenue, GDP, approval and better prices. Peace also needs both sides to agree; war needs only one.

Will an AI nation accept my peace offer?

AI nations answer within a few minutes and judge the offer on how the war is going. They accept when they are losing — land lost, outmatched, low approval — or when a war has dragged on, and refuse when they think they are winning. Warlords are the most stubborn; merchants and isolationists make peace most easily. If they refuse, you'll get a notification saying why, and they reconsider every couple of hours as the war changes. AI nations that are losing may also offer peace themselves.

How do I message other players?

Use Mail (the envelope at the top, or More → Mail on phones), or the Message button on any leader's profile. Mail is private. Use World Chat for public conversation.

How do I mention someone in chat?

Type @ followed by their leader name — for example @Zeiro — or tap @ Mention on their profile. They get a notification, and messages that mention you are highlighted. Tap any name in chat to open that leader's profile.

What is the World Times?

A daily front page written from everything that happened in the last 24 hours: the biggest headline, war and peace, diplomacy, politics, the fastest-growing economies and the great powers. Find it in the menu under The world.

What is the World Council?

The world's parliament. Any nation of Mayor rank or above can propose a resolution for $100,000; every nation (including AI) gets one vote; it passes with a majority and at least 3 votes in favor. Voting lasts up to 6 hours, but closes early as soon as the result can no longer change (counting AI nations and players active in the last 3 days who haven't voted yet), so nobody waits around. There are 21 resolutions in four groups.

Measures against a nation

  • Impose sanctions: the target becomes a rogue state for at least 24 hours.
  • Trade embargo (24 h): no world market and no trade agreements.
  • Arms embargo (36 h): no new warships or aircraft, and recruiting costs double.
  • Freeze foreign assets (24 h): a quarter of its treasury is frozen, then returned.
  • Formal condemnation: −10 approval, −8 stability, +20 aggression, and AI nations shun it for 24 hours.
  • Nuclear inspections (72 h): a warhead still being assembled is dismantled and no new one can be started. Only for nations with a nuclear program.

Support for a nation

  • Ceasefire order (12 h): no battles, strikes or nuclear launches between the target and its enemies. The wars stay declared.
  • Security guarantee (48 h): its provinces defend 20% better, and attackers gain double aggression.
  • Humanitarian aid: every nation that votes yes sends 1% of its treasury (up to $2M) and 5% of its food (up to 300). The target also gains health and stability.
  • Lift sanctions: ends any embargo, arms embargo, asset freeze, condemnation and rogue status, and lowers aggression to 30. Not within 72 hours of a nuclear strike.

Worldwide treaties

  • Climate accord, Arms reduction treaty, Free trade pact, Peacekeeping mandate (24 h each).
  • Non-proliferation treaty (48 h): no new nuclear programs or warheads.
  • Global health initiative (24 h): health rises and pandemics are half as deadly. Every nation chips in 0.5% of its treasury.
  • Science cooperation (24 h): research is 25% cheaper.
  • Food security program (24 h): +20% food output, food 15% cheaper.
  • Debt relief: 20% of every nation's debt is forgiven, and inflation is 1 point higher for 24 hours.

Pacts and your own resolutions

  • Collective defense pact (72 h): everyone who votes yes becomes a member. If anyone attacks a member, every other member automatically declares war on the attacker. Choose whether it covers attacks by AI nations only or by anyone. A member who attacks a fellow member is expelled, and a member allied with the attacker still goes to war (the alliance ends).
  • Custom resolution: write your own (up to 200 characters) and choose 24, 48 or 72 hours. If it passes it is listed as in force with every nation that supported it. The game cannot enforce typed text, so it is a public commitment. AI nations do not vote on it.

Measures on a nation show as 🏛 badges on its profile, and on your Overview if they apply to you.

My nation is a rogue state or under sanctions. How do I get back to normal?

Stop attacking: aggression fades by 6 points an hour, and rogue status ends once at least 24 hours have passed and your aggression is below 50.

Or petition the World Council from the World Council page. It's free, works at any rank, and you can petition once every 12 hours. If a majority votes yes (with at least 3 yes votes), your embargo, frozen assets (returned to you), condemnation, sanctions and rogue status end at once, and your aggression drops to 30.

AI nations vote yes only if you haven't attacked or struck anyone for 12 hours, and nations you're at war with vote no, so make peace first. A petition isn't possible within 72 hours of launching a nuclear strike.

Does the World Council act on its own?

Yes — the world takes notice. The Council automatically puts a resolution to the vote when a nation:

  • launches 3 or more attacks in 6 hours, or its aggression reaches 35 → Formal condemnation
  • attacks 3 or more different nations in 12 hours, or its aggression reaches 60 → Sanctions (passing makes it a rogue state)
  • tests a nuclear weapon → Formal condemnation
  • starts assembling a launchable warhead → Nuclear inspections
  • is hit by a nuclear strike → Humanitarian aid for it

Counter-attacks against a nation that hit you first don't count. Every nation still votes, so nothing passes without a majority — make your case in chat! The same nation can face each kind of automatic vote at most once a day.

What are world events?

Every 10–18 hours a global event hits every nation at once for about 8 hours — Global Recession, Pandemic, Oil Crisis, Commodity Boom, Technology Revolution, Crop Failure, Financial Crisis or an Era of Peace — and every leader gets a decision on how to respond.

Intelligence

How do spies work?

Train agents in Operations, then launch covert operations from the Intelligence page:

  • Steal technology (3 agents, $150k): copy research from a more advanced nation.
  • Sabotage industry (2 agents, $100k): destroy stockpiles, damage infrastructure.
  • Incite unrest (2 agents, $100k): their stability −10 and approval −5.
  • Interfere in election (3 agents, $200k): swing a democracy's next election — or your own.

Success depends on your agents versus their counter-intelligence, technology and fortifications. Fail, and your agents may be caught publicly: your aggression rises and relations sour.

Account, chat & fair play

Can I have more than one account?

No — one account per person and per email address. Accounts that look like duplicates are flagged for the administrators and can be banned.

I forgot my password.

Send a message to the game administrator, who can set a new password for you. Once you're back in, change it on the Account page.

How do I change my password, sign out of other devices, or delete my account?

Open the Account page — the person icon at the top of the screen, or More → Account on phones. You can change your password (this signs out your other devices), sign out of every device, or permanently delete your account, nation, chat messages and mail.

Can people cheat?

Every action is checked and calculated by the game server, so results can't be changed from a browser. Suspicious activity is flagged automatically for the administrators.

Someone is being rude. What can I do?

Open their profile (tap their name) and choose Mute — you'll no longer see their chat or receive their mail — or Report, which goes privately to the administrators. Offensive language is filtered automatically. See the Terms for the rules.

How do I invite friends?

Open the Account page and copy your invite link. When a friend joins through it and founds a nation, you get $1,000,000 and they get $500,000 (up to 20 friends). Rewards aren't given for accounts on your own network.

Can I share my nation?

Yes — every nation has a public page like worldpowersgame.com/n/your-nation-name. Open a nation's profile and tap Share to copy the link.

I found a bug. How do I report it?

Tap Report a bug on the Account page, the Guide or the More menu on phones. Describe what happened and attach a screenshot if you can — your device type and any errors are included automatically and go straight to the administrator.

Are there special events?

Yes. Besides random world events, the administrators run special events such as Double XP, double income or faster operations weekends. A green banner shows while one is running, and everyone is notified when it starts.

Is it free?

Yes, completely free.
